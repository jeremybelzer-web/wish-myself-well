# Momentum

The heart of the app (Jeremy's words #14 to #16): the feeling that a film is going somewhere important. An audience can pay attention to only one thing at a time, and when what holds it keeps moving between different curiosities, the film stays rich and engaging. Momentum shows how long attention rests on each kind of curiosity, warns when one runs too long, and records the cue that moved it on.

Open it from **Library, Momentum** (once `<script src="momentum/load.js"></script>` is in `index.html`), or on its own page: `momentum/index.html`.

## What you see

- **The tabs, in five groups**: every tab sits under a group's name, so you can tell where to start. **See it** (Attention, Story drive, Who we watch, Momentum curve, Comedy timing) looks at how attention moves through a film. **Fix it** (Compass, On the engine, Pace it, Cue lab, Panel timing) gives ideas and changes for your own film. **Learn it** (Learn it, Film rates, Momentum notes, Watch and tap) teaches the idea and shows the films it is measured against. **Play it live** (Pads, Perform) moves attention while a film plays. **Share it** (Cue sheet, Report) makes a page or a sheet for other people. On a wide screen the group's name sits over its tabs; on a phone it sits beside them, and nothing scrolls sideways. The group of the open tab is marked, and a short line under the tabs says what that group is for. The first time, a **Start here** line points at Learn it; it goes away once you open Learn it or press Hide (`localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-start-v1`). The last open tab is remembered as before. A file adding a tab can name its group (`addTab({ ..., group: "see" | "fix" | "learn" | "live" | "share" })`); without one, a known tab goes to its own group and a new one to See it.
- **The meter**: how long one family of curiosities has held attention, against a limit. Green means fresh, yellow means getting long, red means too long. With "My film, live" it follows what you play or perform, and it keeps climbing while nothing changes. For any other film, point at the timeline to see the meter at that moment.
- **Four numbers**: the momentum reading (how hard whatever holds attention pushes the story, worn down when it stays too long), how many times a minute attention moves, the usual rest, and the longest rest.
- **The ring (pie chart)**: what share of the time each family held attention. Eight families have their own color; the other five are gray and listed under "Other".
- **What moved it on**: the share of each cue (visual, audio, thought, movement, plot), plus quiet cues, where something stops: the music cuts out, a silence falls, someone goes still.
- **The timeline**: who held attention when, a letter for the cue at each move, and a line that climbs while one family holds attention and drops when attention moves. The red part is past the limit.
- **How attention moves**: every move from one family to another, counted: how many times, the cue that made it most often, how often it came from something stopping (a quiet cue), which curiosities took attention, and the cue the films you compare with use for the same move (measured films only). This is Jeremy's question of which action or non-action moves attention, and to where. The Compass uses the same counts: when your measured films make a move mostly on one cue, it suggests that cue. Beside the Screen's Player each meter also says where attention came from and on which cue.
- **Held too long**: every stretch past the limit, with a different kind of cue to try.
- **Compared with**: your film against a film you love, or the average of several, in plain sentences.
- **Film rates**: the default curated list (Claude's estimates, marked as estimates) and any traced film you measure.
- **Momentum notes**: every curiosity's note on how it moves the plot forward, how it builds the themes, how it pulls attention onward, and one thing to try. This covers clothes and landscape too.

- **Compass** (the Prism Compass): where attention should go next. A needle points at the family to move to, chosen from how often your films move from the current family to each other one (once you have measured a traced film), how much more time your films give each family, how long since it last held attention, and how hard it pushes the story. The top three come with reasons, a curiosity to try, and **Make this move on My film**, which steps one live control of that family by one notch.
- **On the engine**: the engine's timeline (Library, Engine) as the audience would watch it, with an attention lane and a cue lane, one cell per moment. Every stretch past the limit gets a suggested engine link: the curiosity holding attention leads one from another family, so its next change moves attention on. **Add this link to the engine** is an ordinary engine command, one undo step.
- **Perform**: follow My film live and send the meter out while you perform. MIDI control changes go out (attention CC 20, momentum CC 21, family CC 22, compass CC 23; the channel and numbers can be changed), with a note each time attention moves (60 visual, 61 audio, 62 thought, 63 movement, 64 plot) and note 72 past the limit. The desktop app's bridge sends the same values (OSC `/curio/value/m/attention`, `m/over`, `m/momentum`, `m/family`, `m/compass`, 0 to 1, and the same over WebSocket). A phone buzzes past the limit, and a full-screen **stage meter** can be read from across a room.
- **Beside the Player** (the Screen, CapCut layout): a Momentum panel docks at the right edge of the Player and follows its playhead. It shows a meter for My film and one for each inspiration film in the Player: the family holding attention at that moment, how long it has held it against the limit (● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long), and a ribbon of the whole film colored by family. Click a ribbon to move the playhead. Under them the Compass says where to move attention next, compared with the films on screen (untick to use the Momentum window's list), and **Make this move here** writes a node at the playhead: one curiosity of that family one notch along its scale, one undo step. **›** folds it to a slim meter; **Open** opens the whole Momentum window. In the "Player on the right" layout and on a phone it sits under the Player. It reads My film the way the Player does (one value per curiosity: Master, then Camera, then the characters), so it agrees with the Player's "Attention:" label.
- **Pads** (for performers): a grid of big pads, one per attention family (its color and its letter), for My film (the engine's film, the one the Screen plays). Press a pad (click it, a key 1 to 9, 0, - or =, or a note on a MIDI keyboard, pad controller or wearable) and attention moves to that family at the moment playing now: the family's strongest curiosity that can change there gets one notch, one Undo step per press. The moment is the Screen's playhead, or a moment you pick. Record a take: each press lands at the moment playing then, and stopping the take turns all its presses into one Undo step. A barometer (● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long) shows how long the family at that moment has held attention, and the pad the Compass suggests next is marked ★. Learn note under a pad makes it answer to the note you hit. One MIDI connection is shared with Perform.
- **Pace it**: pace My film (the engine's film, the one the Screen plays) like a film you love, or the average of the films you compare with. It plans a few small changes: where one family holds attention past the limit (or much longer than your films allow, when your film moves attention much less often), one new change at about the moment your films usually move on, to the family the Compass suggests; where attention moves far more often than in your film (it may feel rushed), a moment left to rest, marked **Let it rest** (one curiosity kept where it was instead of changing). Each change says when, what changes and why, with a tick box. A table shows the numbers now and with the ticked changes (moves a minute, the usual and longest stretch on one family, the momentum reading), read again in memory without touching the film. **Gentle**, **Medium** and **Strong** plan up to 2, 4 or 8 changes. **Apply the ticked changes** is one engine step ("Momentum: pace like ..."), so one Undo takes them all back.
- **Panel timing** (in Fix it): how long each panel of a storyboard scene could stay on screen. Today every panel lasts the same time (Seconds per panel). Attention gets tired when one kind of curiosity holds it too long and fresh when it moves, so each panel gets its own time by a few plain rules (`RULES` in `timing.js`): the first panel gets 1.25 times the usual time so the audience settles in; a panel where attention moves to a new family gets 1.5 times so the new thing lands; while one family keeps attention, ● Fresh keeps the usual time, ▲ Getting long gets 0.75 times and ■ Too long gets half, counted on the suggested times; a comedy payoff (a payoff, callback or topper from Comedy timing) gets half a second more; and every panel stays between 0.5 and 8 seconds. A bar per panel shows its time against the usual one, with the family's letter, and a list says why each panel gets its time. The scene's length today and with this timing sit on top. **Play with this timing** flips through the panels in a small preview at the suggested times (it stops at the end, with Stop, or when you change tab or close the window). **Use this timing in the storyboard** gives each panel of the scene its suggested time in the Storyboard itself (`panels[i].seconds`, through the storyboard's own `CuriosityStoryboard.setTiming(list, "Momentum: panel timing")`), so its flip book holds each panel that long; it is one save and one Undo step. **Clear the timing** gives the scene's panels back to the flip book's Speed slider (also one Undo step). Beside the suggestion the tab shows the storyboard's current time for each panel (`CuriosityStoryboard.timing()`): a gray number under each bar ("Speed" when the panel follows the Speed slider), a line under each reason, and a tile that says whether the storyboard already uses this timing. **Download the timing** saves it as JSON (`format: "curiomatic-panel-timing"`, `version: 1`, `panels: [{ panel, seconds, family, curiosity, label, kind, payoff, why }]`) or CSV. Its own setting (the scene picked): `curiosities-momentum-timing-v1`.
- **Comedy timing** (comedy is central): the comedy rhythm of any film. It finds every setup, payoff, topper, cutaway and callback (from "Setup and payoff", "Kind of joke", Callback, Running gag, The topper, Cutaway gag, "Oddly specific", and any stretch where comedy holds attention) and says, in plain sentences, how often the jokes come, how long a setup waits for its payoff, how many payoffs get a beat of stillness or a quiet cue before the punchline, how long a laugh gets to land before attention moves on, and which jokes are callbacks. A strip of the film marks setups (S), payoffs (P), other jokes (J) and callbacks (C), with an arc from each setup to its payoff and a ? on a setup that never pays off. Suggestions name the payoffs that need a pause and the setups that never pay off; on the engine's film, **Add this pause on the engine** writes one more notch of "Pause before the punchline" at that moment, one undo step. It compares with Paddington 2, Hot Fuzz and The Grand Budapest Hotel (Claude's estimates, marked, kept in comedy-timing.js, not rates.js) and with any film you measure there. Measured films: `localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-comedy-v1`.
- **Momentum curve**: a graph of momentum over the whole film, one value from 0 (stalled) to 5 (surging) for every moment: how hard the curiosity holding attention pushes the story, worn down while one kind of curiosity stays past the limit, smoothed over 15 seconds (8, 30 or 60 to choose). ▲ marks where the film surges, shaded stretches are sags (momentum under 2 for at least 8 seconds), and the film is split into a beginning, a middle and an end, each a third of its length. One curated film can be laid over it as a gray dashed line, stretched to the same length so the shapes compare. Point at or tap the line to see the moment, its value and what held attention. Under the chart every sag says when it happens, how long it lasts, what held attention and one thing to try (from the Compass at that moment); on the engine's film, **Go to moment N on the Screen** moves the Screen's playhead there.
- **On the Storyboard** (where you flip through panels): under each scene's panels (All scenes) and under the flip book's small pictures (Flip through) an attention strip has one cell per panel, colored by the family holding attention there. A letter marks where attention moved to a new family and the cue that moved it (V visual, A audio, T thought, M movement, P plot; a dot first means a quiet cue). ▲ marks a family getting long and ■ one past the limit (point at a cell for the words). Click a cell to see that panel in the flip book. Under the flip book's buttons a small meter follows the panel on show, also while it plays: the family, how long it has held attention against the limit, and where it came from. Each panel lasts the **Seconds per panel** set here. The strip redraws when the Storyboard tells it that it redrew or turned a page (`CuriosityStoryboard.on`); an older Storyboard without that is watched for changes instead.
- **Momentum in the automation** (`sources.js`): three modulation sources that any automation patch or lane can follow, the way it follows an LFO, a knob or MIDI. See "Momentum in the automation" below.

- **Learn it**: a guided walk-through that teaches momentum with any film (the first curated film to start). It steps through the film one moment at a time with **Back** and **Next** (or the left and right arrow keys). Each moment shows the kind of curiosity holding attention as a colored chip with its letter, a small barometer of how long it has held against the limit (● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long), and two or three plain sentences: what took attention and on which cue, how long the last thing held, and **Try this in your film** from that curiosity's momentum note. Every few moments a quick check asks a question with three choices (such as "What took attention here?"), says kindly whether the answer was right, and keeps a score until the page reloads. **Every moment** lists them all to jump to; **Skip the quiet holds** keeps only the moments where attention moved. For the engine's film, or a curated film shown in one of the Screen's inspiration viewers, **Watch this moment** closes the window and moves the Screen's playhead there. The last moment for each film is remembered (`localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-lesson-v1`).

- **Who we watch**: attention by character, for the engine's film (the only film with a track for each character). Whose change took attention at each moment, so you see the share of the time each character holds attention (a bar each, with seconds), the longest time each one goes unwatched (● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long), who never takes attention, a strip of who holds attention moment by moment (click a moment to move the Screen's playhead there), the families each one pulls attention with, and plain warnings such as "Ida has not held attention for 40 seconds". Master and Camera count as the film itself. By default a character may go twice the limit (at least 30 seconds) unwatched.
- **Story drive**: Jeremy's rule that every curiosity must drive the plot and the themes forward. Every time attention moves to a new kind of curiosity, it says whether the story moved too, and how: **P** plot (a want, a secret, the stakes, a reveal, a turn), **C** character (a feeling, a step on their arc, a mind changing), **T** theme (something that held attention earlier comes back changed: an echo), or **D** decoration only (only the look or the sound changed). One sentence on top ("6 of 7 times attention moves, the story moves too"), a strip of the film with a color and a letter for each move, the share of moves that drive the story, the longest decoration-only stretch in seconds, the themes that echo, and every move with its reason. Each empty stretch (moves in a row that only decorate) suggests a story family to bring in, from the Compass; on the engine's film **Make this move here** changes one curiosity of that family by one notch at the start of the stretch, one undo step. The films you compare with get the same reading side by side when they have a trace; a film with only an estimate gets no number. The rules: a change pushes the story when it is about the plot, a feeling or a character's thinking, or when its momentum note says it pushes the story 4 or more out of 5 (camera, light, clothes, the set, effects, cuts and music never do on their own); a move drives the story when the curiosity that took attention pushes it, or one that does changes in the same moment or the next 2; arcs, types, mindsets and focus are character, a plot cue is plot, other feelings and thoughts are character; an echo is a curiosity that held attention before, has not for 30 seconds or more, and comes back with a new value (a camera or cut coming back does not count). A beat that sets a curiosity's own "Pushes the story" slider decides for it, and "Tied to a theme" at "clearly" or more makes it a theme. These are guesses written down to be argued with (`DEFAULTS` in `drive.js`).
- **Three films** (Jeremy's three-window layout): two films that inspire you and your own film, as three attention ribbons one above the other (defaults: the first two curated films and the engine's film). "Same length" stretches every ribbon to the full width so their shapes compare; "Real time" puts all three on one clock. Under each ribbon: moves a minute, usual hold, longest hold (● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long) and momentum from 0 to 5. Point at or tap any ribbon and one line crosses all three at the same point, with a line of text per film saying what holds attention there and which cue brought it. **Blend** lists 3 to 5 moves for your film taken from both films: where both move attention at about the same point and your film holds, the move and the cue they use (when they disagree, it says so and offers both). On the engine's film, "Make this move here" makes it, one undo step.
- **Report**: one page about any film in the picker, to show other people (an animator, a teacher, a class), compared with the films picked in Attention: the title, one plain paragraph, the ring of time per family, the barometer (the hold at the end and the longest hold, ● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long), the momentum curve (the Momentum curve's own line, or a simple line from the reading), the moves of attention that happen most and their cues, the three longest stretches past the limit, and the Compass's next move. **Print or save as PDF** prints only the report; **Download as a web page** saves one self-contained `.html` file (inline pictures and styles, no scripts, nothing from the internet); **Copy the summary** puts it on the clipboard as plain text. Counts only: never notes or script lines.
- **Cue sheet**: an exposure sheet for momentum, the table animators plan timing on, for any film in the picker. A frame is one still picture, and 24 of them make one second (25 or 30 can be picked). One row per moment: its time, its frames (first to last; a moment of Seconds per panel lasts that many seconds times the frames a second, so 3 seconds is 72 frames at 24; a curated film keeps its own times), what holds attention (the family's color and letter, and the curiosity), the cue that moved it there (visual, audio, thought, movement or plot; blank where attention stayed), how long it has held so far (● Fresh, ▲ Getting long, ■ Too long), and a short note for the animator from the numbers, such as "Hold: attention is getting long on Camera; plan a change by frame 312". Frames start at 1, as in Maya. **Download the sheet (CSV)** opens in any spreadsheet. **Download for Maya (JSON)** saves the format below. **Copy for Maya** copies a short Python script: pasted into Maya's Script Editor (Python tab) and run, it adds one Time Slider bookmark per change of attention, named after the family, the curiosity and the cue (`cmds.timeSliderBookmark`, or Maya's own bookmark module), and where bookmarks are not there it makes a locator per change instead (`momentum_cue_0001` and so on, with a `momentumNote` text attribute and its visibility keyed on from that frame). It only adds: it never deletes, moves or renames anything, and it does not move the time slider. Set the Maya scene to the same frames a second first. The Maya panel (`maya/scripts/curiosities_maya/`, `import curiosities_maya; curiosities_maya.show()`) loads its scripts as a package from Maya's scripts folder; a later step could read this JSON there instead of pasting the script. The rate and "Only the moments where attention moves" are remembered (`localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-cuesheet-v1`).

  The JSON for Maya (`format: "curiomatic-momentum-cues"`, `version: 1`): `film` (its title), `fps`, `startFrame` and `endFrame` (the whole film), `limitSeconds`, `moments` (one per row: `moment`, `startFrame`, `endFrame`, `family`, `curiosity` (its id), `label`, `change` ("start", "move", "shift", or null when attention stayed), `cue`, `quiet` (true when the cue is something stopping), `heldSeconds`, `status`, `note`) and `cues` (one per change of attention: `name` (`momentum_cue_0001`...), `frame`, `family`, `curiosity`, `label`, `cue`, `quiet`, `color` (the family's color, `#rrggbb`)). Frame numbers are whole numbers, end frames included.
- **Cue lab**: Jeremy's question of exactly which action or non-action moves attention from one kind of curiosity to the next, studied across the films he loves. Every curated film with a trace is read the same way as your film (none get made-up numbers; a film with only an estimate is listed as "no trace yet"). A table shows each film's share of moves made by each cue (visual, audio, thought, movement, plot) and how many moves were a stop (a silence, a pause, someone going still) against an action, with your film's row marked. **Your film versus your favorites** says, cue by cue, whether your film uses it much less, about the same or much more than the average of the films you compare with (under 0.75 times is much less, over 1.33 times is much more, the same lines as Compared with). **Steal this move** lists the 5 moves from one family to another that the curated films make most and your film never makes, with the cue they use; on the engine's film **Make this move here** changes one curiosity of that family by one notch where attention has held longest, one undo step. A bar chart shows how many times a minute attention moves in each film.
- **Watch and tap** (in Learn it): measure a real film yourself, since most curated films have only Claude's estimates. Play the film anywhere (a video file from your computer plays right in the tab and is never uploaded; for a film on another screen the tab has a stopwatch with Start and Pause) and tap the kind of curiosity that holds your attention each time it changes: 13 pads with each family's color and letter, keys 1 to 9, 0, -, = and [. Before a tap, pick the cue that moved it (keys V, A, T, M, P, or N for none) and hold Shift or turn on **Something stopped** when a stop moved it (a quiet cue). A live ribbon and a line with ● Fresh, ▲ Getting long or ■ Too long follow your taps; **Undo last tap** (or Backspace) takes one back. The taps become one beat every few seconds (Seconds per panel by default, or the beat length picked there): each beat gets the family that held attention for most of it and the cue that moved attention there. **Save as a curated film** adds it with the title you type (`CuriosityStudy.add`, `source: "watched and tapped"`, counts and ids only, no notes), so Film rates (press Measure), Cue lab and Three films read it as a measured film. Several people can tap the same film: **Average with an earlier tapping of the same title** keeps every tapping and gives each beat the family most of them tapped. Each family move is written as one change of one stand-in curiosity of that family whose note gives the chosen cue, so the attention reading gives back exactly what was tapped; where no curiosity of a family gives that cue, its usual cue is used. Unsaved taps: `localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-tap-v1`.

- **Feeling road**: each character's emotional road, and the film's, laid under one attention ribbon of My film (the engine's film, the one the Screen plays). Each road is a small line, one point per scene, on the feeling scale from calm to charged (dreamlike, melancholy, loving, curious, absurd, joyful, anxious, fearful, triumphant, angry: the engine's own order, `CurioScale`), and each scene sits over its share of My film's moments. It lists, in plain sentences: **flat feelings**, where a feeling stays the same for 3 scenes or more (2 to 5 to choose) while attention also stalls (it never moves to a new family inside the stretch), each with a suggestion to turn the feeling in the middle of the stretch one step up or down the scale, toward the feeling the road reaches next, and why; **feeling turns that attention misses**, where a feeling jumps 3 steps or more (2 to 5) in a scene where attention never rests on Feeling, so the audience may not notice; and how many feeling turns come in a scene where attention also moves. The roads come from the story store (the **Emotional road** workspace: Feeling, then Emotional road, one feeling per character per scene, and one for the film). While no feeling is set there, it says how to fill it and reads the engine's feeling lanes instead: Emotion on the Master track is the film's road, Emotion on a character's track is that character's, one point per moment. **Turn ...** writes a suggestion back where its road came from: a story road through the story store's own `applyRoad`, undone by its `undoRoad`; an engine road through `CurioEngine.send` as one batch (a plain value, or a hand edit on that one cell when the column has automation points), one engine undo step. The Screen today reads only the engine (the story store feeds the Storyboard, the Prism and the workspaces, not the Screen), so only a change to an engine road shows on the Screen. **Undo** in the tab takes the last one back. Its own setting: `curiosities-momentum-feeling-v1`.
- **Audiences** (See it): different audiences tire of different things at different speeds. The film stays the same; each audience stretches or shrinks the film's limit (a limit multiplier) and has its own patience for each family (a multiplier per family). Six starting guesses, clearly labeled as guesses to adjust: Young kids (short limit, more patience for Comedy and Movement, less for Lines & voice), Film students (longer limit, more patience for Camera and Light & color), Festival crowd, Family audience, Phone scrollers (very short limit) and Theater crowd (more patience for Lines & voice). Pick a film and up to three audiences: each gets the attention ribbon with ■ marks where it gets tired (the part of a family stretch past its own limit), the share of the film it spends past its limit, and a plain sentence ("Phone scrollers lose interest 5 times, first at 0:12 on Camera"). **Who is this film for?** ranks every audience by the fewest tired stretches. A slider per family changes an audience (Put back the starting guess undoes it), and **Make my own audience** copies one under your own name. Kept in `localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-audience-v1`.

## Films it can read

My film live, My film's panels, the engine's timeline, any storyboard scene, the whole storyboard, and every curated film (your studies, shared traces, and the made-up practice scenes). Panels have no clock, so **Seconds per panel** sets how long each one lasts (3 by default).

## How attention is worked out

1. At each beat, every curiosity whose value changed could take the audience's attention.
2. Its pull is the size of the change (steps along its scale), times how strongly its family draws the eye (faces, voices and plot pull hardest), nudged up by how much it pushes the story.
3. The strongest one takes attention if its pull is big enough. Otherwise attention stays where it was. The same curiosity changing again keeps attention where it is.
4. Attention stays until something else takes it. A family that holds attention past the limit gets a warning. By default the limit is 2.5 times the usual family stretch in the films you compare with. You can set your own.

These are guesses about how people watch, kept as plain numbers (`FAMILY_PULL`, `SHIFT_MIN` in `attention.js`) so they can be tuned or replaced by measurement.

## Momentum in the automation

`sources.js` gives the automation (Library, Automation patch bay, or the Automate part of any workspace) three new things to follow. Each one is a number from 0 to 1, like an LFO:

| Source | In the automation window | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `source:momentum:attention` | Momentum: attention held | How long the kind of curiosity holding attention has held it, against the limit. 0 means it just took attention; 1 means it has reached the limit (it stays at 1 past it). |
| `source:momentum:level` | Momentum: how much it moves | The momentum reading, 0 (stalled) to 5 (surging), shown as 0 to 1. |
| `source:momentum:over` | Momentum: past the limit | 1 while that family is past the limit, else 0: a gate that switches on when attention has stayed too long. |

How to use one: open a patch (any curiosity, suite or proximity). In its **Moved by** row, next to LFO, Knob and MIDI CC, press one of the three Momentum buttons, then set **Depth** for how far it moves the patch (100% is all the way). Press the patch's trigger to run it. A lane inside a patch can follow one too: pick it in that lane's **moved by** list. In a saved patch it is written as `mod: "source:momentum:attention"` (or `momentum:level`, `momentum:over`). Some ideas: let the camera push in further the longer one thing holds attention (`attention`), let the music swell with the momentum reading (`level`), or flick a cut, a light or a sound on when attention has stayed too long (`over`).

What it follows: while **Perform** runs, its live meter (what you play or perform on My film). Otherwise My film (the engine's film, the one the Screen plays) at the Screen's playhead, with the seconds per panel, limit and films to compare with from the Attention tab. It reads the film once after each change of the film or of the playhead and keeps that number, so following it costs nothing on each frame. With an older automation that has no `addSource`, nothing is added.

Saved choices: `localStorage` key `curiosities-momentum-v1`.
