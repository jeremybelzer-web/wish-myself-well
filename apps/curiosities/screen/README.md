# The Screen

Curiomatic's main layout. It was first modeled on Final Cut Pro (Jeremy, 2026-10-02, 17:18Z to 17:24Z). Now it is laid out and colored like **CapCut** (18:00Z: "I'm leaning towards mainly using CapCut... I think their user interface is great"). It is dark, with four panels:

- **The library, top left** (CapCut's Media panel). The seventeen categories are a row of icon tabs: Camera, Performance, Light & color, Filters, Set, Wardrobe, Audio, Text, Effects, Transitions, Speed, Editing, Feeling, Comedy, Character, Story, Page. A sidebar of pills holds the category's groups: its workspaces, then its suites, sparks and elixirs. The cards are in a grid. Click a card to look through it. Its cyan **+** puts it on the timeline: a curiosity becomes a track, a suite puts all its curiosities on, and a spark is added to your film. **Search every curiosity** looks across all categories.
- **The Player, in the center.** One viewer for each inspiration film (a curated film, played as a cheap animated storyboard) and one for your own film. It starts with one inspiration film; **+ Inspiration film** adds as many as you like, and × removes one. Under the viewers sit the timecode (one moment is one second), Play, the speed, and the window layouts: **1 / 2 / 3** (your film alone, one inspiration film and yours, or two and yours), plus **Side by side** or **Stacked**. The lens buttons (Highlight, Overlay, Lens only, Off) are in the Player's header.
- **Details, on the right** (CapCut's Details panel). It shows the category picked in the icon row for the viewer you picked (click a viewer, or its Inspect button). Main curiosities come first (the ones that push the story hardest), and **Show all** opens the rest. Each curiosity has its main control and a fold (▸ 6) with its fine controls: toggles, lists, stepped sliders, knobs, and a small chart of how it moves through the film. Each category also suggests a layout (Use 3 windows for Light & color).
  - On **your film**, every control writes a node at the playhead.
  - On an **inspiration film**, tick **Take into my film** on any curiosity and set an amount. Take the same curiosity from several films, or different ones from each, then **Blend**. Each film pulls your film toward it by its amount, so two films at 50% meet halfway. A blend is one undo step.
- **Looking through** (the bar): pick a curiosity, a suite, a spark or an elixir. Every viewer lights up what it is about (**Highlight**), writes its values on the picture (**Overlay**), draws only it (**Lens only**), or shows the plain picture (**Off**). Yellow dots on each scrub bar mark where it happens in that film.
- **Character** (`character.js`): the character matrix's curiosities (its 18 axes, Enneagram health and dramatic role) on each character's own lanes. Details start with a character bar: pick whose lanes the controls write to, set their Enneagram type, **+ Character**, **Bring the matrix's cast in** (the cast built in the Archetype workspace becomes character tracks), and **Open the 3D matrix**, which opens the matrix over the Screen linked to your film: its cast is the character tracks, its scenes are the moments, and every change in it is a node on the timeline (one undo step each; undo on the timeline and the matrix follows). An axis with no lane follows the character's type at that moment's health, so a lane appears only where a character departs from their type.
- **The timeline**, full width along the bottom: film clip tracks on top (each inspiration film's beats, then your film's moments), then curiosity lanes running left to right, with nodes joined by lines.
  - Click an empty spot to add a node. Drag a node up or down to change it, sideways to move it. Hold Alt or Shift while dragging to copy; hold ⌘/Ctrl to drop it without snapping to a marker. Double-click a node to remove it (two presses on the same node; never adds one first).
  - **Lines, the Ableton Live way** (Jeremy, 2026-10-04; the gesture list is in `/mnt/project-files/decisions/ableton-lane-gestures.md`): hovering a line thickens it and shows what it plays there. **Click a line** to add a node at the moment you click, at the line's own value (nodes sit on whole moments, so it goes to the nearest moment between the line's two nodes). **Drag a line** up or down to move both of its nodes together; no node is added, and it is one undo step. **Alt (Option) + drag a line** curves it as you drag: up bows it upward, down bows it downward, further bows it more; the result is the same curve the Curves pop-up shows. **Right-click a line** for Add a node here, Ease in, Ease out, Ease in and out, Straight line, Curves…, Shapes ▾ (the area Shape menu over that line) and Simplify this lane (takes out nodes that change nothing); right-click a node for Remove, Copy spark and Simplify. A tap and a drag work the same with a finger. A locked lane (🔒) takes none of these.
  - **Master lane and its automation group.** Every curiosity's lane is its master lane. Its header has an **ON/OFF** switch (the lane's automation plays or not; its nodes stay) and a two-way switch: **⌐ Steps** (an on/off lane: straight up or down at each node, then flat to the next) or **⟋ Lines** (free nodes and lines from 0 at the bottom to 100 at the top). Under the name, the **▸ N settings** tab unfolds one sub-lane for every setting in the curiosity's window (⧉), **How much (0 to 100)** (its Amount) first, indented under the master; press it again (or ⌥K with the lane picked) to fold them. Groups start folded; a folded group shows small dots along the bottom of the master lane where its settings have nodes. Open groups are kept per curiosity in the timeline's tools (`curiosities-screen-tools-v1`, `subs`). A sub-lane edits exactly like any lane and goes on its master's track. `CurioLanes.automationGroup(id)` lists them; `segMoveCommands`, `bendFrom`, `simplifyCommands` and `EASES` are the pure parts; the mounted lanes have `subs(id, open?)`, `simplify(laneKey)` and `curveLine(segKey, shape, bend)`.
  - **Drop a node on a node in another lane** to join them: a spark, drawn as an orange line. Joined nodes move and copy together; a group of several joins is an elixir.
  - **Copy spark** takes the picked node and everything joined to it; move the playhead and **Paste** puts it in another scene.
- **Arrange** (the bar, or Library, Arrange): the timeline on its own beside Details, the library and Player hidden unless you tick Show the player. Every automated curiosity is a track with a dropdown to change which curiosity it is (its nodes keep their place on the new scale). **Show all potential curiosities** and **Show all potential curiosity suites** list everything by category; pick a category or a suite to lay its tracks out.
  - **Graded lanes.** The dropdowns (+ Add a curiosity track and each track's own) list a curiosity's graded lanes right under it, indented: the 3D staging lanes Eyelines: speaking now, Where they stand: how they stand together and sitting or standing, Character path: walks to, Shot size: who it frames and Setting: where it happens, plus any slider the database marks `lane: true`. Each shows only when the database grades it in this build (`CurioScale.known("eyeline.speaking")`), so the Screen works with or without the 3D characters. A curiosity whose bare lane the app only knows as off or on (`CurioAuto.domain`), such as Eyelines, offers its graded lane (`eyeline.setting`) under its own name and keeps the bare lane last, marked "(bare lane)". Show all potential curiosities shows the graded lanes too. A graded lane each character has (Eyelines' graded setting and speaking now, sitting or standing, walks to) goes on every character track when you add it, as one undo step; Who it frames goes on the camera track, the film-wide ones on Master (`CurioLanes.laneParts`, `partTrack`).
  - **One row per track.** When several tracks carry the same curiosity (each character's Character path), each track's lane is its own row, named by the track ("Character path · Maya", "Character path · Sam"), with its own Off, Solo and Lock, in its category's fold. Clicking a row, or a node on it, makes Details read and write that track's lane (its header says "Character path on Sam"); otherwise Details uses the character picked in the Character tab if that character carries it, else the first track. The character matrix's own curiosities still follow the Character tab's pick. Area copy and paste, suite clips, templates and a track's dropdown keep to each row's own track; Look ▾ keeps each track's own setting of a shared curiosity (`byTrack`) and gives it back to that track. Films where each curiosity is on one track look as before.

- **Curiosity-first, automation-first CapCut panels** (Jeremy, 18:02Z: "integrating these screens into our app. Only make them all curiosity-centric and automation-centric... incorporate some of the things that Maya does... we want it to be accessible"). Every pick lands as nodes on automation lanes at the playhead. A picked curiosity card opens across the grid with its settings as tiles, like CapCut's effect thumbnails; a tile drops that setting as a node. A card's **+** puts the curiosity on the timeline with a node at its current setting. A suite's **+** drops every member at the suite's own setting in one undo step. Two extra tabs lead the icon row: **My film** (everything automated so far and the sparks joined in, like Maya's Outliner or CapCut's Yours) and **Templates** (every suite by category and the elixirs as linked recipes, CapCut's Templates tab).
- **Favorites and Recently used** (CapCut's star on any effect, and its Recently used list). Every library card (curiosity, suite, spark, elixir) has a small ☆ at its top right; click it (or tab to it and press Enter) to star the card, and again to take the star off. The star is its own button, so clicking it never picks the card. **★ Favorites**, the first tab in the icon row, shows **Recently used** at the top (the last 12 different things you picked or put into your film with +, newest first) and then **Starred** (everything you starred, in the order you starred it, or "Star anything in the library to keep it here."). Recently used is a section of the Favorites tab rather than a tab of its own, so the things you reach for most are all in one place; its pill in the sidebar scrolls to it. Search covers them too: in the Favorites tab it searches only your favorites and recently used (suites and sparks included); in any other tab, matching favorites are listed after the matching curiosities. Both lists are kept in `localStorage` key `curiosities-screen-faves-v1` as `{ faves: ["level|id"], recent: ["level|id"] }`, across reloads; they are not part of your film and never an undo step. An id the database no longer has is skipped quietly. `CurioScreen.faves` has `now()`, `items(which)` and the pure steps `toggle`, `used` and `clean` for tests.
- **From Maya, in plain words**: a key diamond in front of every control in Details (Maya's channel box): filled when there is a node at this moment, hollow when the lane has nodes elsewhere, faint when it is not automated yet; click to set or take off a key. Each lane's header switches between **Glide** (a straight line from node to node, Maya's linear curve), **Smooth** (eases out of each node and into the next, Maya's spline; needs the engine's smooth mode, PR #30, and skips to Jump without it) and **Jump** (hold each setting until the next node, Maya's stepped curve). **Ghosts** in the Player (or G) show the moments before and after faintly over my film (Maya's ghosting). **I** (or CapCut's ⇧↩) and **O** set a play range that Play loops over (Maya's playback range), dimmed outside on the timeline; ⌥X or the Loop button clears it. The picked curiosity in Details shows its **momentum**: how hard it pushes the story, what it does for the plot and theme, how it pulls attention, and something to try.
- **The timeline toolbar** copies CapCut's: Select and Split tools, + Moment and − Moment (add a copy of the playhead's moment or take it out, with everything after sliding along; see Adding and taking out moments), Undo, Redo, Copy, Paste, Remove node, Marker, the main track Magnet (moving a node moves every later node in its lane), Snapping (a node dropped next to a marker lands on it), Linkage (joined nodes move together; off, a node moves alone and its lines stretch; its ⚙ opens Linkage settings, modeled on CapCut's box: tick which kinds of joined node, by category, move, copy and paste with the node you grab, and whether Delete takes them too), Preview axis (hover to see a moment in the Player) and zoom. Its settings and markers are kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1`.
- **Markers** (CapCut's markers): a flag on one moment of the film with a color (red, orange, yellow, green, blue or purple) and a short note, such as "the joke lands". Marker (or M) puts one at the playhead. Double-click or right-click its flag on the ruler to write the note, pick the color or delete it (Esc closes the pop-up). The flag is drawn in its color, the note shows when you hover over it and is written beside it when there is room. **Markers ▾** lists every marker (moment, time into the film, color dot, note); click one to move the playhead there and scroll it into view, or ✎ to edit it. Markers are kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `[{ row, color, note }]`; an older save that kept a plain list of moment ids loads as orange markers with no note (`CurioLanes.migrateMarkers`). **⇧[ and ⇧]** move the playhead to the previous or next marker, as in CapCut, and the timeline scrolls to it; when there is no marker that way, the timeline's status line says so ("No marker after moment 7.", or "There are no markers yet"). Pure helper: `CurioLanes.markerStep(markers, rows, moment, direction)`.
- **Mark the turns** (auto markers, like CapCut's Beats, which puts markers on a song's beats so you can cut to them): a button in the Markers ▾ list that puts a marker on every moment where the film turns. **Purple** where what holds the audience's attention moves from one family to another (the momentum reading, `CurioAttention`, the same one behind the Player's "Attention:" label), with a note such as "attention moves from camera to feeling". **Red** where the feeling of the moment (the Emotion curiosity) changes: "feeling turns from joyful to anxious". **Yellow** where a track on the timeline jumps by more than half its range: "shot size jumps from wide to extreme close-up". One marker per moment; when a moment turns in two ways, the notes are joined. A moment that already has a marker of your own keeps yours exactly as it is. Pressing it again replaces the old auto markers instead of adding more. **Clear auto markers** takes off only the auto ones. Auto markers are kept with an `auto: true` flag (kept through `migrateMarkers`) and tagged "auto" in the list; writing a note on one or changing its color makes it your own. The rules are a pure helper, `CurioLanes.turnMarkers(beats, rows, { attention, lanes })`, with `mergeTurnMarkers` and `clearAutoMarkers`; the Screen hands the lanes its values per moment through the `beats` mount option.
- **The Attention track** (like the sound waveform CapCut draws under the clips, so you can see the sound's shape while you edit): a thin band right under My film's clip track that you look at but don't edit. For each moment it shows a colored block for what holds the audience's attention there, one color per attention family (Camera, Feeling, Lines & voice, Plot & character...). Hover over a block for the family and the curiosity, and the band's name carries a small key of the colors. A filled white line on top shows how strongly the film pulls forward at each moment. That is the push of whatever holds attention (how hard it moves the story, out of 5), worn down once one family has held attention past the momentum reading's limit, the same rule as the reading's overall momentum. If the reading gives no push, the line shows how much of the film changes from one moment to the next instead. Click the band to move the playhead to that moment. It follows zoom and scroll like the other tracks and redraws whenever the nodes change. The toolbar's **Attention** button shows or hides it. It starts on, and the choice is kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `attention: false`. Without the momentum code it draws nothing and says so in one plain line. The reading is `CurioAttention` (the `attention` mount option, else the page's), fed by the `beats` mount option; the pure helper is `CurioLanes.attentionTrack(beats, { attention, secondsPerBeat })`.
- **Layout** (the bar, like CapCut's layout menu): Player in the middle (the default), Library full height, Details full height, or Player on the right (a tall Player column beside the library, Details and the timeline).
- **Export ▾** (the bar, like CapCut's big Export button at the top right). The beta exports storyboards only, and everything is made on your device: nothing is uploaded. Files are saved with names that start with `curiomatic-`.
  - **Storyboard sheet** opens a printable page in a new tab: every moment of My film as a frame in the current Ratio shape, its moment number and clock time, the marker's note (with its color) if the moment has one, and a short line of what changed since the moment before ("Shot size: wide → close-up · Emotion: joyful → anxious and 2 more"). Pick 2, 3 or 4 frames per row, then Print. If the browser blocks the new tab, the sheet is saved as an `.html` file instead.
  - **This frame as a picture** saves the playhead's frame of My film (the plain picture, without the lens) as **PNG** (1280px on its long side) or **SVG**.
  - **Settings list (spreadsheet)** saves a CSV: one row per moment (moment, time, marker note), then one column per curiosity that has a value anywhere, with plain labels in the header and plain values in the cells. It opens in any spreadsheet app.
  - The pure builders are `window.CurioScreenExport`: `sheetHtml(data)`, `csv(moments, { keys, label, text })`, `changes(prev, cur, opts)`, `frameSvg(svg, shape, px)` and `fileName(film, what, ext)`; `tests/run.js` checks them with no page.
- **History ▾** (the bar, like the History panel in editing apps): every change you can undo, newest first, under a **Now** line, and every change you can redo, over it (the next one nearest Now), by their plain names. The newest step is where your film is now. Click an older step to go back to just after it, or a redo step to go forward to just after it: the Screen presses undo or redo that many times through the same path as ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z (the app-wide undo list when the page has one, `CurioStore`, else the engine's own), so it is exactly like pressing ⌘Z that many times, and the status line says how far it went. **Before all of these** undoes every listed step. At most the last 30 steps show, then "and N earlier" (redo the same, "and N later"); with nothing on either list it says "Nothing to undo yet." The list keeps up while it is open, whenever the film or the app-wide list changes. It opens with Enter, the arrow keys (and Home, End) move through the steps, and it closes on Esc (focus goes back to History ▾) or a click anywhere else. It changes nothing by itself and saves nothing. The pure part is `window.CurioScreenHistory`: `rows({ undo, redo }, max)` (both lists as `history()` gives them, oldest undo first and next redo first) returns `{ undo, redo, earlier, later, all }` with each step's plain name and how many undos or redos reach it, and `plain(label)` drops the engine's "Engine:" prefix; `tests/run.js` checks them.
- **Shortcuts** (the bar, or press ?): CapCut's whole Shortcut window (Timeline, Player, Basic and Other tabs) and its toolbar tooltips, each doing the nearest thing here: ⌘B split, ⇧⌘B split all, A select, B split mode, [ and ] step between nodes, ⇧[ and ⇧] previous and next marker, P magnet, N snapping, ~ linkage, S preview axis, M marker, ⌘+ and ⌘− zoom, ⇧Z fit, J K L shuttle, Q and W delete left and right, ⌥M add a moment here, ⇧⌥M duplicate moments, ⌥⌫ take out moments, ⇧⌥K add a node, Space play, ⇧⌘F full-screen Player, ⌥⇧+ ⌥⇧− ⌥⇧Z Player zoom, ⌘⌥R rulers, ⌘; the thirds guide, ⌘C ⌘X ⌘V, ⇧⌘C and ⇧⌘V copy and paste a node's setting, ⌥⌘C and ⌥⌘V copy and paste a whole moment's look (Look ▾ below), ⌫ delete, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z. Keys with nothing to do here yet are listed, faded. ⌘ is Ctrl on Windows.
- **Quick find** (⌘K, Ctrl+K on Windows, or the 🔍 in the bar; like CapCut's search and an editing app's command palette): one box, a few typed words, results in groups. **Curiosities** (each with its short plain description; picking one looks through it the same way clicking its card does: the library moves to its category with the card picked, Details shows it, and its folded lane group opens on the timeline), **Suites**, **Actions** (every Screen shortcut by its name with its key shown, plus Export ▾ and its items, History ▾, Guides, Compare, Captions, Look ▾, Mark the turns, Markers ▾, layouts, views, lens, Player windows and the picked curiosity's ⋯ menu; each runs the same function or button) and **Moments and markers** (a marker by its note, or "moment 12"; your own marker notes come before a curiosity that matches the same word). Every typed word must be found, in any order; a word that starts a word of the name counts most, then letters inside the name, then the description; groups come best match first. ↑ ↓ (or Tab) move, Enter or a click picks, Esc or a click outside closes. With nothing typed it shows the last 8 picks (localStorage `curiosities-screen-find-v1`, `{ recent: [ids] }`), or a few to try. While it is open no key reaches the film: `inToolWindow` says so and the box stops every key. ⌘K was free (plain K stops playing, ⇧⌥K adds a keyframe). The pure matcher is `window.CurioScreenFind` (`norm`, `score`, `rank`, `remember`, `clean`, `isKey`); `tests/run.js` checks it.

## Your own curiosities, suites and sparks

Jeremy: "The user should be able to define and create their own curiosities as well as curiosity suites and sparks. Either here or in a similar space." So the library makes them, in `mine.js` (`window.CurioMine`) and the "My own curiosities" block of `ui.js`.

- **Where.** Every category tab of the library starts with a bar: **+ New curiosity** (in the Suites group **+ New suite**, in Sparks **+ New spark**; when the category has no suites or sparks yet, those buttons sit beside it), then **Export mine** and **Import…**. Quick find (⌘K) has New curiosity, New suite and New spark too.
- **A curiosity**: its name, *What it is*, *How it moves the story forward and the audience's attention*, *What to try*, its category (one of the library's tabs; it starts on the tab you are in), what it catches first (the eye, the ear, the mind, movement, the plot) and how hard it pushes the story (0 to 5), and its scale: **named steps** in order ("calm → tense → frantic", or with commas) or **a number range** with a unit (0 to 200 bpm). **+ Add a slider** gives it finer parts, each its own lane: a name and its steps or range in one box ("slow, steady, fast" or "0 to 10 km/h"). The three descriptions become its momentum note, so Details' Momentum box and the Momentum panel read them.
- **A suite**: a name, what it is, a category, and two or more curiosities picked from a list you narrow by typing (yours and the database's), each at a setting or at any setting.
- **A spark**: one sentence of pickers, "When *Tension in the room* *goes up / goes down / changes / becomes…*, then *Shot size* *goes up / goes down / changes too / becomes close* within *2* moments", shown back as plain words while you make it, plus a name if you like (the sentence otherwise), what it is and a category. It is stored the way the database stores sparks (`when` and `then`, each a curiosity with `change` rises, drops or changes, or `is` a value, and `within` moments).
- **Then** each is a card like any other, with a small cyan **mine** mark and a **✎** under its star. Its **+** works like any card's: a curiosity goes on the timeline with a node at the playhead (its lane is named by it and plays in the engine, its extra sliders are graded lanes in Arrange's pickers), a suite drops every member at its setting, and a spark becomes a rule in your film (the library's **+** on a database spark now adds it too: it asked the engine for the wrong link ids before). Details shows and edits it like any curiosity, Quick find finds it (tagged "Mine"), and the levels (`CurioLevels.resolve`, `fires`) read your suites and sparks.
- **✎** opens the same form to change it; **Export this** saves it as a `.json` file (a suite or spark brings the curiosities of yours it uses), and **Delete…** asks first, saying what goes with it ("It is in your film on 1 lane; that lane comes out too. Your suites and sparks "The calm before the storm" will skip it."): **Delete for good** or **Keep it**. Deleting a curiosity that is in your film takes its lanes out first (its own undo step).
- **Undo.** Making, changing, deleting or importing is one step on the app-wide undo list (⌘Z, History ▾), because what you make is a part of the shared store (`CurioStore.part("screenMyCuriosities")`). If you change the steps of a curiosity that is in your film, its nodes move to the same place on the new steps.
- **Sharing.** Export mine saves everything as `curiomatic-my-curiosities.json` (format `curiomatic-my-curiosities`, version 1). Import… adds what is new: the same item again is skipped, a different item with an id you already use gets a new id (its suites and sparks follow it), and members or ends naming curiosities this app doesn't know are left out, all said in one message.
- **Saved** in `localStorage` key `curiosities-user-curiosities-v1` as `{ v: 1, curiosities, suites, proximities }`, so project files and autosave carry it like every other `curiosities-*` key. Ids are always `my-` and the name in small letters and dashes ("my-tension-in-the-room"), with -2, -3... when taken; no database row starts with `my-` (`tests/run.js` checks), and an imported id that doesn't start with `my-` is given one that does, so yours can never land on a database row.
- **How it reaches everything.** At load and after every change, `CurioMine.install` registers each item with the curiosity database through its own adders (`CuriosityDB.curiosity`, `.suite`, `.proximity`), then makes the row exactly yours: the curiosity's workspace is the first workspace of the category you picked, `main: "setting"`, tag `mine`. Deleted ones are taken out of the database's lists. The engine's spark pack (`window.CURIOSITY_LINKS`) is rebuilt with `CuriosityDB.links()`. The engine learns scales from `window.CurioScale` (engine/catalog.js), which reads the curiosity list once when it loads and remembers every answer, so for `my-` ids `mine.js` answers `CurioScale` itself (domain, known, label, fix, pos, at, step, steps, start) and leaves every other id to the engine. The engine reads its saved film before the Screen's files load, so it drops nodes of yours on a reload; `CurioMine.start()` reads the saved film again once yours are registered (`rescue()`). See "Hooks the engine could add" below.

**Hooks the engine could add** (so `mine.js` would not have to stand in for them; `engine/` and `data/` belong to other threads):

- `engine/catalog.js`: `CurioScale.register(id, { kind: "choice", options } | { kind: "range", min, max, step }, { label, group })`, `CurioScale.unregister(id)` and `CurioScale.forget(id)` (drop a remembered answer), checked in `domain`, `known`, `label`, `group` and `start` before `byId`.
- `data/curiosity-db.js`: `CuriosityDB.remove(level, id)` to take a row out of its list and its index.
- Load order: the engine reads its film in `engine/ui.js` (`E.load()`) before `screen/load.js` runs. Loading `screen/mine.js` from `index.html` before `engine/load.js` (or the engine calling `E.load()` on the page's `load` event) would let your nodes come back on the first read.

## How it is built

- Nothing new is saved for your film: lanes, nodes and joins are the engine's (`engine/state.js`) automation points and links, so undo, saving and the fingerprint check cover them. A join is a link with `scope: { from, to }` naming its two moments, `from.is` the first node's value, `does: "set"` the second's, and `within` the gap.
- The Screen's own view (which films, layout, open categories, takes) is `localStorage` key `curiosities-screen-v1`; the spark clipboard is `curiosities-screen-clip-v1`; saved suite clips are `curiosities-suite-clips-v1`.
- The editing rows now live in `data/db-editing.js` (moved there by the database thread, PR #7): 60 editing curiosities, 17 suites, 40 sparks and 7 elixirs taken from the Final Cut Pro User Guide and CapCut (including CapCut's transition and effect families; Superpowers, Hallucination, Clone, outline and Mood body effects; Retro, Glitch, Distortion, Party and 3D video effects; filter families, LUTs, templates, the stock library, end cards, translated voices, generated shots and voice cleanup). Since then the Main layout thread added 10 story and momentum curiosities of its own at the end of the file (How the scene ends, Questions the audience is holding, A clock running out, The opening grab, Ends where it starts, Match cut, Who knows first, Music sting, Something new every, Chapter cards) with 3 suites, 6 sparks and 1 elixir. Body Mood effects feed the Emotion curiosities both ways: each feeling brings its mood sticker, and the stickers move the feeling's sliders (elixirs "The face tells the feeling" and "The mood moves the feeling"); the Mood sticker curiosity shows under Feeling as well as Text. They cover transitions, filters and adjustments, text and captions, speed and timing, the audio mix, layers and masks, and the frame. `index.html` loads it right after `data/db-momentum.js`, before `CuriosityDB.install`.

## Lane heads: Off, Solo and Lock

Like the small buttons on CapCut's track headers (hide or mute a track, lock it) and an audio app's solo. Each lane's name has three small buttons in `lanes.js`. To save room they fold away until you hover over the lane's name or tab to them. They always show on touch screens, and whenever one is in use.

- **👁 Off**: turns the lane's automation off or back on (the engine lane's own `on`, one undo step). An off lane stops changing the film. Its nodes stay, drawn faded with a dashed line, and its name is crossed out.
- **S Solo**: plays only this lane's automation and turns every other lane off, in one undo step. Press it again and the others come back the way they were. What was on before is kept in the tools as `solo: { lk, was }`. If the film changed in between, only lanes that still exist are put back, and lanes made since are left alone. Soloing another lane keeps the first "before". The soloed lane has a yellow S and a yellow edge. Pure helpers: `soloCommands(st, laneKey, prev)` and `soloActive(st, solo)`.
- **🔒 Lock**: stops the timeline from changing that lane's nodes. Clicking can't add a node, and nodes can't be dragged, removed, split or curved. Area tools, Move, Remove and Paste skip a locked lane and say so in a short message. A locked lane can still be copied, turned off or soloed. A lock is a view setting, not film data. It is kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `locks: { "track|curiosity": true }`. A locked lane has a hatched background and a lit 🔒. Details' controls still write to a locked lane, because the lock covers the timeline only.

Off and Solo need a lane that has nodes (an engine lane). Lock needs the lane to be on a track. The mounted lanes also have `laneOff(lk)`, `solo(lk)` and `lock(lk)`.

## A row's ⋯ menu (Apply to all and Reset)

CapCut's Details panel has "Apply to all" and a Reset for each section. Here every curiosity row in Details (on My film) has a small **⋯** beside Fine-tune with four choices, each one undo step:

## Guides over the picture

Like the guides CapCut's Player can show. **Guides ▾** sits next to Ratio in the Player's transport bar. Tick any mix of them; they are drawn over My film's frame only, in its frame shape (a vertical frame gets vertical guides), and never get in the way of a click on the frame. Hover over a guide in the menu for one line on what it is for.

- **Thirds**: two lines each way at a third and two thirds. Put the important thing where the lines cross; pictures feel more alive than when it's dead center.
- **Center cross**: a small cross in the exact middle.
- **Safe areas**: the action-safe box (90% of the frame) and the title-safe box (80%), labeled "keep action inside" and "keep words inside".
- **Golden ratio**: lines at about 38% and 62% each way, a little nearer the middle than thirds.
- **Where attention is**: the rest of the picture dims a little around a soft glow on the part the moment's attention sits on, with a line such as "Eyes on: the main character's face". It uses the momentum reading's family for the moment (`CurioAttention`, the same as the "Attention:" label). `CurioFrame` doesn't report where it drew things, so `guideSpot` works the spot out from the moment's values with frame.js's own placement rules. Shot size, camera height, how many people and a left composition decide where the main character's face and body are. An insert shot means the object. A feeling, a thought or a plot turn glows on the face. ## Transitions between moments

Like CapCut's Transitions tab, made for a storyboard. My film plays a moment at a time, like a flipbook, so a transition sits on a **join**: how moment N hands over to moment N+1. Each join gets one of six, each with a one-line tooltip:

- **Cut** (the default, how it always worked): the next picture appears at once, with nothing in between.
- **Fade**: the picture melts into the next one.
- **Wipe**: the next picture slides in from the left like a curtain and covers this one.
- **Push**: the next picture shoves this one out of the frame, sideways.
- **Zoom**: this picture rushes toward you and fades, and the next one is behind it.
- **Match**: the drawing stays put and only the settings that change move into place (a shot size steps through the sizes between, a number counts up; anything not on a scale changes halfway).

And a **length**: a quarter, half (the default), three quarters or a whole moment.

Three ways to set it:

1. **The ◇ on the timeline.** My film's clip track has a small ◇ at every join; it fills in once the join has something other than a Cut, and its tooltip names it. Click it (or tab to it and press Enter) for a small chooser: the six kinds, Length and Use on every join. Esc or a click elsewhere closes it.
2. **Details.** On My film, a **Transition** row under the header is for the join *into* the moment at the playhead ("how moment 4 hands over to moment 5"). On moment 1 it says there is no join before it.
3. **Use on every join** (in both) gives every join of the film the same kind and length.

It plays in the Player whenever the playhead steps one moment across a join: with Play (its length is a part of how long Play holds a moment, so at 1× a half-moment Fade takes 0.55s), with ◀ ▶, and when you scrub along the strip or the timeline. The picture going out is laid over the new one and moved by CSS (opacity, a clip, a slide or a scale; Match redraws the one storyboard picture with blended settings, cached), so it costs one extra frame while it runs. A jump of more than one moment just cuts. Only My film plays transitions; inspiration films are left as they are.

Each change is **one undo step** on the app-wide list (⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z and History ▾ name it, "Transition into moment 3: Fade, half a moment"); Use on every join is one step too. Transitions are the Screen's own setting, not the engine's film: they are kept in `localStorage` key `curiosities-screen-transitions-v1` as `{ joins: { "3": { kind, len } } }`, keyed by the number of the moment the join leads into (cuts are not kept). They are a part of the shared store (`engine/store.js`, part `screenTransitions`), which is what puts them on the undo list; without the store they are still saved but not undoable. The database's **Transition style** curiosity is a different thing, a value per moment that holds until the next node, and is left as it is. They are keyed by moment number, so when the timeline adds or takes out moments (see Adding and taking out moments, below) it renumbers them in the same undo step. Adding or removing a moment anywhere else (the Engine window's own rows) does not move them.

**Export** lists them: the Settings list gets a **Transition in** column (once any join has one; "Cut" elsewhere), and each frame on the Storyboard sheet that doesn't come in on a cut says "Comes in with: Fade, half a moment".

The pure part is `window.CurioScreenTransitions` (`KINDS`, `LENGTHS`, `clean`, `at`, `set`, `all`, `label`, `list`, `blend`, `style`); `CurioScreen.transitions` has `now()`, `at(into)`, `set(into, kind, len)`, `all(kind, len)`, `preview(into, p)` (holds the Player at progress p of the join, for tests) and `playing()`. `lanes.js` draws the ◇ from the `joins` mount option.

## Adding and taking out moments (ripple)

Like CapCut's Split and Delete with the main track magnet on: the clips after the change slide along so there is never a gap. Here the clips are moments. My film's moments are the engine's rows (`engine/state.js`, at most 64); the Momentum window's seconds per moment (`secondsPerPanel`) only says how long each one lasts, so the number of moments is changed with the engine's own `addRow` and `removeRow`, sent from the Screen.

- **+ Moment** (the timeline toolbar, **⌥M**, or ⌘K "Add a moment here"): a copy of the playhead's moment goes right after it, and the playhead moves onto the copy, ready to change. The copy plays exactly what the moment plays: its nodes, its own settings and its hand edits are copied, and where a lane's line would now play something else (a glide that got one moment longer) a node with what it played before goes in, so nothing jumps.
- **Duplicate moments** (the area tools while a stretch is selected, **⇧⌥M**, or ⌘K): a copy of every selected moment goes right after the stretch, on every lane (not only the selected lanes), and the copies are selected. Joins inside the stretch are copied with it. With no stretch selected, ⇧⌥M copies the playhead's moment.
- **− Moment** and **Take out moments** (**⌥⌫**): the playhead's moment, or the selected stretch, comes out of every lane and everything after slides earlier to close the gap. The nodes on those moments go with them, and so does a join that touches one of them (left alone it would become a rule for the whole lane). Where a lane's line around the gap would now play something else, a node keeps what it played. **Remove nodes** still clears only the selected lanes and keeps the moments.

Everything kept per moment moves together, in **one undo step** (⌘Z, the toolbar's Undo, History ▾):

- **nodes** on every lane are kept by moment, so they slide along by themselves;
- **joins** that cross the change get their gap ("within") made longer or shorter;
- **markers** are kept by moment too; a marker on a moment taken out goes;
- **transitions** and **words on the frame** are kept by moment number, so they are renumbered. A copy comes in on a cut, the transitions inside a duplicated stretch are copied with it, and the moment after a gap keeps the way it came in. Words showing on the playhead's moment run on over its copy; words wholly inside a duplicated stretch get a copy on the copies; words over moments taken out get shorter, and words wholly inside go;
- the **play range** (I and O) grows, slides or shrinks the same way.

Suite clips and templates keep their moves relative to their own start, and film lines are worked out from the film's length, so none of them needs changing. Curves are kept by their two nodes and redraw as long as the points between still match. A rule link (a spark that is not a join between two nodes) reacts to the new seam the way it reacts to any change, so a duplicated stretch can play a little differently where a rule fires on the cut into it.

**How one undo covers it all.** The engine's batch goes on the app-wide undo list as usual (`CurioStore.external`). The transitions and texts are changed through their store parts with `{ record: false }`, a new `replace` command each, so they add no step of their own. The Screen listens to the engine, and when that very step is undone or redone (by ⌘Z, History ▾, the toolbar, or the engine's own undo) it puts the transitions, texts, markers and play range back with it. The timeline toolbar's Undo and Redo now use the app-wide list too (the same as ⌘Z), so they never undo half a ripple. What an owner could add to make this simpler: a way for a store part's step and an engine step to be grouped as one entry (for example `CurioStore.group(label, fn)`), or an engine `send(msg, { share: false })` so the Screen could push one combined step itself.

**Locked lanes (🔒).** A ripple moves the whole film in time, so a locked lane's nodes slide along with their moments like everything else, but nothing is ever added to or taken off a locked lane. A copied moment gets no node there (its line runs on across the copy, and the message names the lane), and taking out a moment that has a node on a locked lane is refused with a plain message ("Shot size is locked (🔒) and has a node on moment 3...").

The film holds 64 moments, so adding stops there with a message, and the last moment can't be taken out. The pure part is `window.CurioScreenRipple`: `insert(st, a, b, h)` and `remove(st, a, b, h)` build the engine batch (`h = { S: CurioScale, locked(lk), maxRows, maxLinks }`), and `joins`, `texts`, `markers` and `range` move the Screen's own per-moment things; `laneValues` and `freshIds` follow the engine's own rules. `CurioScreen.ripple(kind, { a, b })` runs it ("add", "duplicate" or "delete"); `CurioLanes.setMarkers(list)` puts back a whole marker list. `node screen/tests/run.js` checks the plans and that nothing jumps; `screen/tests/browser.js` checks the buttons, keys, ⌘K, one undo and redo, and the locked lane.

## Sparks from outside: MIDI, speech and the camera (the Catalyst window)

From the music app's notes (section 7, and section 8 items 2 to 5), translated to film (`triggers.js`, `window.CurioTriggers`; the difference list is `/mnt/project-files/reference/music-vs-film-triggers.md`). Right-click (control-click on a Mac) a node, a lane's name (the curiosity as a suite: its master lane and its settings' lanes), a suite card, a suite clip or a master node: **Assign On Spark…** opens one spark, when + does + limits.

- **When**: a MIDI note or CC (Learn, or type the number); any control in the app (**Map…**, then click it: the click maps it and does not press it; every control is registered through one call, `mappable(id, label)`); an event on a track: a cut, a line of dialogue (a character's Speaking now turns to speaking), a character entering frame (Body in frame = enters), a panel turn, or only one chosen event ("Only the one at moment 5"); the film reaching a section (each marker starts a section that runs to the next; the stretch before the first is the Opening); an oscillator every 1, 2 or 4 moments; speech (the browser's own free speech recognition, or typed words where there is none); the camera (movement on the left, the right, up high or anywhere, read in the browser only, never uploaded, off until switched on).
- **Does**: on, off, on / off, scale by % (the same as a master node: 0 % is off, the curiosity's start value; 50 % is halfway; 100 % plays as drawn), set a value, follow a value, press (one moment). Each except press can **latch**, act **while held**, or **toggle**.
- **Limits**: only between two values (velocity or value, in %); every time, the first N times or every Nth time (counted again each time playback starts); several separate ranges of moments; only in, or never in, chosen sections; only while playing.

A firing is a performance, not an undo step: it goes on the engine's perform layer (`CurioEngine.perform("triggers", layer)`), which the viewers, the playback and the export all read, and it is put back when playback stops (or with **Put everything back**). Assigning, changing or removing a spark is one undo step (a store part saved under `curiosities-screen-triggers-v1`). A locked lane (🔒) is left as it is. The bar's **Sparks** lists every spark (ON/OFF, Edit, ×, how often it fired); **Spark** opens the Curiosity Spark window: MIDI, Listen and typed words, the camera with its meters, and how close each spark is to firing, live.

Hooks: `CurioTriggers.trigger(id, on, value)` fires one spark from any other input; `input(key, down, value)` feeds a source ("midi:note:60", "control:<id>", "body:left"); `forTarget("mnode:<id>")` lists a master node's sparks for its Spark view; a master node is driven through `CurioMasters.trigger(id, on, { scale })` (on null = back as drawn).

## Resizing and folding panels, and undo for the view

Jeremy, 2026-10-04: "Each window should be resizable. The main view window, the control window to its right, all borders should be draggable and resizable ... The user should be able to completely collapse any window, and then hovering the cursor over that spot should show the user they can drag that border out ... using a small triangle or triangles", and "UNDO - undo should be able to undo absolutely anything. Even dragging windows around."

**Borders.** Every border between the Screen's panels can be dragged: library | Player, Player | Details (in every layout from the Layout menu, and in Arrange), the panels above | the timeline, the Player | the side panels column beside it (the Momentum column), the library's group list | its cards, and the top of the Whole film strip (its frames get taller). Hovering a border shows the resize cursor, a cyan line along it and small triangles (◂ ▸ or ▴ ▾) pointing the ways it can go. With the keyboard: Tab to a border, then the arrow keys (Shift for 60px steps, Home and End for the smallest and biggest size). Each panel has a smallest size (library 200px, Details 220px, timeline 110px, side panels 150px, group list 84px, strip 24 to 160px) and the Player always keeps at least 240 by 140px. Double-click a border to put its panel back to its usual size.

**Folding a panel away.** Drag a border past half its panel's smallest size, click the small « (or ») that shows on the border while you hover it, or press Enter on a focused border. The panel folds away completely and leaves a thin edge. Hovering the edge shows the resize cursor and one triangle pointing the way to drag it out; drag it out to the width you want, or click it (or Enter) to bring the panel back at the size it had. The Whole film strip folds into its "▸ Whole film" line.

Sizes are kept in the Screen's view (`curiosities-screen-v1`, `sizes: { lib, insp, tl, dock, side, ov, shut: { panel: true } }` in pixels), so they come back after a reload; on a smaller window they are fitted so the Player keeps its room. Up to 860px wide (tablets and phones) the panels stack and the page scrolls, so there are no borders there and the stylesheet's sizes are used.

**⧉ windows** can be resized too: drag the corner at their bottom right, or Tab to it and use the arrow keys.

**Undo for the view.** Your film, transitions and words on the frame were already undo steps on the app-wide list (`CurioStore`). Now the Screen's own view is too, each change one step on the same list, so ⌘Z, ⇧⌘Z, the timeline's Undo and Redo and History ▾ take them in order, by plain names:

- panels: "Resize the library", "Collapse Details", "Bring back the timeline", "Reset the size of the library's group list";
- ⧉ windows: "Open the Shot size window", "Move the Shot size window", "Resize the Shot size window", "Close the Shot size window" (undo opens it again where it was);
- the Player: the 1 2 3 windows and + Inspiration film ("Add an inspiration film"), which film a viewer shows and what you take from it, Side or Stack, the lens, Ghosts, the speed, the Player's zoom, rulers, the play range, Guides, Compare (on or off, what it shows, "Move the Compare line"), Captions, the Whole film strip; the Layout menu ("Layout: Player on the right"); Screen or Arrange; which lanes the timeline shows; Arrange's Show all potential curiosities and suites;
- the timeline's own view (`curiosities-screen-tools-v1`): zoom, lane height, folded lane groups, markers (adding, removing, changing one, the automatic markers), locks, Magnet, Snapping, Linkage and its settings, the preview axis, Film lines, the Attention track, and the Select and Split tools.

How it works (`ui.js`, the block "Panels you can resize and fold away"): the view is read just before each gesture (a press of the mouse or a key, the first turn of the wheel, a list being picked from) and again just after it ends; whatever changed becomes one step. So a whole drag is one step, never one per pixel, and key presses on the same border less than 1.5 seconds apart join one step. A gesture that also changed the film (any step of its own on the app-wide list: a node, a ripple, a template) is left to that step, so one undo never takes back half of something: a ripple's markers and play range still come back with the ripple's own step. A gesture that only moved around (picking a card, a category, a lane, a viewer) is not a step, and neither is a change made with no gesture at all (by another part of the app). Undoing a view step puts back only what that step changed.

Not undo steps: the playhead and playing, which card, category or lane is picked, the library search, scrolling, and things that only last a moment (menus, Quick find, the shortcuts sheet). The Momentum column's own Hide and Open, and the Character tab's colours and types, belong to those parts and keep their own saving.

For other screens: `CurioScreen.splitter(el, { axis, grow, size, min, max, foldable, folded, set, reset, name })` makes any element such a border (pointer, keys, folding, triangles; it brings its own small stylesheet), so the Viewer's panels can use the same one. `CurioScreen.panels` has `now()`, `borders()` and `reset()`; `window.CurioScreenPanels` holds the pure part (cleaning saved sizes, where a drag lands, the grid's columns and rows, each layout's borders, and the plain names of view steps), checked in `tests/run.js`.

## Hooks for other threads

`CurioScreen.row()` is the playhead's moment; `CurioScreen.setRow(i)` moves it. `CurioScreen.on(fn)` is told `{ row, rows, playing }` after every redraw, playhead move and play or pause (`CurioScreen.playing()` says which), and returns a function that stops it. `CurioScreen.addPanel({ id, label, place: "player" | "details" | "timeline", mount(el) })` docks a side panel (the momentum meter, the 3D actors): the Screen makes a `.sc-dock` element in that place, mounts it once and keeps it across redraws; `CurioScreen.removePanel(id)` takes it off. Panels beside the Player stack top to bottom in one column the Screen owns (`.sc-player > .sc-docks`), in the order they were added, so two panels never cover each other; it goes under the Player in the Player-on-the-right layout and on phones. Keys typed inside a tool window (a dialog, `.sc-maya-dlg`, `.rig-dlg`) or while a modal window is open are left to that tool: the Screen's shortcuts and ⌘Z ignore them. The clock uses the Momentum window's seconds per moment (`secondsPerPanel` in `curiosities-momentum-v1`, 3 by default).
