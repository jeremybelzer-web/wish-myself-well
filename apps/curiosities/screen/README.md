# The Screen

Curiomatic's main layout. It was first modeled on Final Cut Pro (Jeremy, 2026-10-02, 17:18Z to 17:24Z). Now it is laid out and colored like **CapCut** (18:00Z: "I'm leaning towards mainly using CapCut... I think their user interface is great"). It is dark, with four panels:

- **The library, top left** (CapCut's Media panel). The seventeen categories are a row of icon tabs: Camera, Performance, Light & color, Filters, Set, Wardrobe, Audio, Text, Effects, Transitions, Speed, Editing, Feeling, Comedy, Character, Story, Page. A sidebar of pills holds the category's groups: its workspaces, then its suites, proximities and proximity suites. The cards are in a grid. Click a card to look through it. Its cyan **+** puts it on the timeline: a curiosity becomes a track, a suite puts all its curiosities on, and a proximity is added to your film. **Search every curiosity** looks across all categories.
- **The Player, in the center.** One viewer for each inspiration film (a curated film, played as a cheap animated storyboard) and one for your own film. It starts with one inspiration film; **+ Inspiration film** adds as many as you like, and × removes one. Under the viewers sit the timecode (one moment is one second), Play, the speed, and the window layouts: **1 / 2 / 3** (your film alone, one inspiration film and yours, or two and yours), plus **Side by side** or **Stacked**. The lens buttons (Highlight, Overlay, Lens only, Off) are in the Player's header.
- **Details, on the right** (CapCut's Details panel). It shows the category picked in the icon row for the viewer you picked (click a viewer, or its Inspect button). Main curiosities come first (the ones that push the story hardest), and **Show all** opens the rest. Each curiosity has its main control and a fold (▸ 6) with its fine controls: toggles, lists, stepped sliders, knobs, and a small chart of how it moves through the film. Each category also suggests a layout (Use 3 windows for Light & color).
  - On **your film**, every control writes a node at the playhead.
  - On an **inspiration film**, tick **Take into my film** on any curiosity and set an amount. Take the same curiosity from several films, or different ones from each, then **Blend**. Each film pulls your film toward it by its amount, so two films at 50% meet halfway. A blend is one undo step.
- **Looking through** (the bar): pick a curiosity, a suite, a proximity or a proximity suite. Every viewer lights up what it is about (**Highlight**), writes its values on the picture (**Overlay**), draws only it (**Lens only**), or shows the plain picture (**Off**). Yellow dots on each scrub bar mark where it happens in that film.
- **Character** (`character.js`): the character matrix's curiosities (its 18 axes, Enneagram health and dramatic role) on each character's own lanes. Details start with a character bar: pick whose lanes the controls write to, set their Enneagram type, **+ Character**, **Bring the matrix's cast in** (the cast built in the Archetype workspace becomes character tracks), and **Open the 3D matrix**, which opens the matrix over the Screen linked to your film: its cast is the character tracks, its scenes are the moments, and every change in it is a node on the timeline (one undo step each; undo on the timeline and the matrix follows). An axis with no lane follows the character's type at that moment's health, so a lane appears only where a character departs from their type.
- **The timeline**, full width along the bottom: film clip tracks on top (each inspiration film's beats, then your film's moments), then curiosity lanes running left to right, with nodes joined by lines.
  - Click an empty spot to add a node. Drag a node up or down to change it, sideways to move it. Hold Alt or Shift while dragging to copy. Double-click a node to remove it.
  - **Drop a node on a node in another lane** to join them: a proximity, drawn as an orange line. Joined nodes move and copy together; a group of several joins is a proximity suite.
  - **Copy proximity** takes the picked node and everything joined to it; move the playhead and **Paste** puts it in another scene.
- **Arrange** (the bar, or Library, Arrange): the timeline on its own beside Details, the library and Player hidden unless you tick Show the player. Every automated curiosity is a track with a dropdown to change which curiosity it is (its nodes keep their place on the new scale). **Show all potential curiosities** and **Show all potential curiosity suites** list everything by category; pick a category or a suite to lay its tracks out.

- **Curiosity-first, automation-first CapCut panels** (Jeremy, 18:02Z: "integrating these screens into our app. Only make them all curiosity-centric and automation-centric... incorporate some of the things that Maya does... we want it to be accessible"). Every pick lands as nodes on automation lanes at the playhead. A picked curiosity card opens across the grid with its settings as tiles, like CapCut's effect thumbnails; a tile drops that setting as a node. A card's **+** puts the curiosity on the timeline with a node at its current setting. A suite's **+** drops every member at the suite's own setting in one undo step. Two extra tabs lead the icon row: **My film** (everything automated so far and the proximities joined in, like Maya's Outliner or CapCut's Yours) and **Templates** (every suite by category and the proximity suites as linked recipes, CapCut's Templates tab).
- **From Maya, in plain words**: a key diamond in front of every control in Details (Maya's channel box): filled when there is a node at this moment, hollow when the lane has nodes elsewhere, faint when it is not automated yet; click to set or take off a key. Each lane's header switches between **Glide** (a straight line from node to node, Maya's linear curve), **Smooth** (eases out of each node and into the next, Maya's spline; needs the engine's smooth mode, PR #30, and skips to Jump without it) and **Jump** (hold each setting until the next node, Maya's stepped curve). **Ghosts** in the Player (or G) show the moments before and after faintly over my film (Maya's ghosting). **I** (or CapCut's ⇧↩) and **O** set a play range that Play loops over (Maya's playback range), dimmed outside on the timeline; ⌥X or the Loop button clears it. The picked curiosity in Details shows its **momentum**: how hard it pushes the story, what it does for the plot and theme, how it pulls attention, and something to try.
- **The timeline toolbar** copies CapCut's: Select and Split tools, Undo, Redo, Copy, Paste, Remove node, Marker, the main track Magnet (moving a node moves every later node in its lane), Snapping (a node dropped next to a marker lands on it), Linkage (joined nodes move together; off, a node moves alone and its lines stretch; its ⚙ opens Linkage settings, modeled on CapCut's box: tick which kinds of joined node, by category, move, copy and paste with the node you grab, and whether Delete takes them too), Preview axis (hover to see a moment in the Player) and zoom. Its settings and markers are kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1`.
- **Markers** (CapCut's markers): a flag on one moment of the film with a color (red, orange, yellow, green, blue or purple) and a short note, such as "the joke lands". Marker (or M) puts one at the playhead. Double-click or right-click its flag on the ruler to write the note, pick the color or delete it (Esc closes the pop-up). The flag is drawn in its color, the note shows when you hover over it and is written beside it when there is room. **Markers ▾** lists every marker (moment, time into the film, color dot, note); click one to move the playhead there and scroll it into view, or ✎ to edit it. Markers are kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `[{ row, color, note }]`; an older save that kept a plain list of moment ids loads as orange markers with no note (`CurioLanes.migrateMarkers`). There is no next or previous marker key: Shift+M would clash with M.
- **Mark the turns** (auto markers, like CapCut's Beats, which puts markers on a song's beats so you can cut to them): a button in the Markers ▾ list that puts a marker on every moment where the film turns. **Purple** where what holds the audience's attention moves from one family to another (the momentum reading, `CurioAttention`, the same one behind the Player's "Attention:" label), with a note such as "attention moves from camera to feeling". **Red** where the feeling of the moment (the Emotion curiosity) changes: "feeling turns from joyful to anxious". **Yellow** where a track on the timeline jumps by more than half its range: "shot size jumps from wide to extreme close-up". One marker per moment; when a moment turns in two ways, the notes are joined. A moment that already has a marker of your own keeps yours exactly as it is. Pressing it again replaces the old auto markers instead of adding more. **Clear auto markers** takes off only the auto ones. Auto markers are kept with an `auto: true` flag (kept through `migrateMarkers`) and tagged "auto" in the list; writing a note on one or changing its color makes it your own. The rules are a pure helper, `CurioLanes.turnMarkers(beats, rows, { attention, lanes })`, with `mergeTurnMarkers` and `clearAutoMarkers`; the Screen hands the lanes its values per moment through the `beats` mount option.
- **The Attention track** (like the sound waveform CapCut draws under the clips, so you can see the sound's shape while you edit): a thin band right under My film's clip track that you look at but don't edit. For each moment it shows a colored block for what holds the audience's attention there, one color per attention family (Camera, Feeling, Lines & voice, Plot & character...). Hover over a block for the family and the curiosity, and the band's name carries a small key of the colors. A filled white line on top shows how strongly the film pulls forward at each moment. That is the push of whatever holds attention (how hard it moves the story, out of 5), worn down once one family has held attention past the momentum reading's limit, the same rule as the reading's overall momentum. If the reading gives no push, the line shows how much of the film changes from one moment to the next instead. Click the band to move the playhead to that moment. It follows zoom and scroll like the other tracks and redraws whenever the nodes change. The toolbar's **Attention** button shows or hides it. It starts on, and the choice is kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `attention: false`. Without the momentum code it draws nothing and says so in one plain line. The reading is `CurioAttention` (the `attention` mount option, else the page's), fed by the `beats` mount option; the pure helper is `CurioLanes.attentionTrack(beats, { attention, secondsPerBeat })`.
- **Layout** (the bar, like CapCut's layout menu): Player in the middle (the default), Library full height, Details full height, or Player on the right (a tall Player column beside the library, Details and the timeline).
- **Export ▾** (the bar, like CapCut's big Export button at the top right). The beta exports storyboards only, and everything is made on your device: nothing is uploaded. Files are saved with names that start with `curiomatic-`.
  - **Storyboard sheet** opens a printable page in a new tab: every moment of My film as a frame in the current Ratio shape, its moment number and clock time, the marker's note (with its color) if the moment has one, and a short line of what changed since the moment before ("Shot size: wide → close-up · Emotion: joyful → anxious and 2 more"). Pick 2, 3 or 4 frames per row, then Print. If the browser blocks the new tab, the sheet is saved as an `.html` file instead.
  - **This frame as a picture** saves the playhead's frame of My film (the plain picture, without the lens) as **PNG** (1280px on its long side) or **SVG**.
  - **Settings list (spreadsheet)** saves a CSV: one row per moment (moment, time, marker note), then one column per curiosity that has a value anywhere, with plain labels in the header and plain values in the cells. It opens in any spreadsheet app.
  - The pure builders are `window.CurioScreenExport`: `sheetHtml(data)`, `csv(moments, { keys, label, text })`, `changes(prev, cur, opts)`, `frameSvg(svg, shape, px)` and `fileName(film, what, ext)`; `tests/run.js` checks them with no page.
- **Shortcuts** (the bar, or press ?): CapCut's whole Shortcut window (Timeline, Player, Basic and Other tabs) and its toolbar tooltips, each doing the nearest thing here: ⌘B split, ⇧⌘B split all, A select, B split mode, [ and ] step between nodes, P magnet, N snapping, ~ linkage, S preview axis, M marker, ⌘+ and ⌘− zoom, ⇧Z fit, J K L shuttle, Q and W delete left and right, ⇧⌥K add a node, Space play, ⇧⌘F full-screen Player, ⌥⇧+ ⌥⇧− ⌥⇧Z Player zoom, ⌘⌥R rulers, ⌘; the thirds guide, ⌘C ⌘X ⌘V, ⇧⌘C and ⇧⌘V copy and paste a node's setting, ⌫ delete, ⌘Z and ⇧⌘Z. Keys with nothing to do here yet are listed, faded. ⌘ is Ctrl on Windows.

## How it is built

- Nothing new is saved for your film: lanes, nodes and joins are the engine's (`engine/state.js`) automation points and links, so undo, saving and the fingerprint check cover them. A join is a link with `scope: { from, to }` naming its two moments, `from.is` the first node's value, `does: "set"` the second's, and `within` the gap.
- The Screen's own view (which films, layout, open categories, takes) is `localStorage` key `curiosities-screen-v1`; the proximity clipboard is `curiosities-screen-clip-v1`; saved suite clips are `curiosities-suite-clips-v1`.
- The editing rows now live in `data/db-editing.js` (moved there by the database thread, PR #7): 60 editing curiosities, 17 suites, 40 proximities and 7 proximity suites taken from the Final Cut Pro User Guide and CapCut (including CapCut's transition and effect families; Superpowers, Hallucination, Clone, outline and Mood body effects; Retro, Glitch, Distortion, Party and 3D video effects; filter families, LUTs, templates, the stock library, end cards, translated voices, generated shots and voice cleanup). Since then the Main layout thread added 10 story and momentum curiosities of its own at the end of the file (How the scene ends, Questions the audience is holding, A clock running out, The opening grab, Ends where it starts, Match cut, Who knows first, Music sting, Something new every, Chapter cards) with 3 suites, 6 proximities and 1 proximity suite. Body Mood effects feed the Emotion curiosities both ways: each feeling brings its mood sticker, and the stickers move the feeling's sliders (proximity suites "The face tells the feeling" and "The mood moves the feeling"); the Mood sticker curiosity shows under Feeling as well as Text. They cover transitions, filters and adjustments, text and captions, speed and timing, the audio mix, layers and masks, and the frame. `index.html` loads it right after `data/db-momentum.js`, before `CuriosityDB.install`.
- `levels.js` (no page): the categories and the four levels (`window.CurioLevels`). `frame.js` (no page): the storyboard frame as SVG (`window.CurioFrame`). `lanes.js`: lanes, nodes, joins, copy and paste (`window.CurioLanes`). `ui.js`: the page (`window.CurioScreen`).
- `character.js` (`window.CharacterScreen`): the Character tab's character bar, which track a matrix curiosity goes on, and the link between the timeline and the 3D matrix (`CharacterMatrix.link`). Each character track's type and colour are kept in `localStorage` key `curiosities-character-screen-v1`.
- `CurioScreen.mountViewer(el, { curiosities, category, film })` puts a small viewer on any other screen, so every screen of the app can have one (Jeremy: "a view window for every single screen of the app").

## Adding it to the app

Two lines in `index.html`: `screen/load.js` after `engine/load.js`, and the editing curiosities after `data/db-momentum.js`:

```html
<script src="data/db-editing.js"></script>
<script src="screen/load.js"></script>
```

It opens on start; `?screen=0` skips it (so do automated browser tests, unless the address has `?screen=1`), and Back to the app closes it (it then stays closed until you open it again).

## Zoom, scroll, copy and paste, curves, and curiosity windows

Jeremy, 2026-10-02 20:26Z (his words #25). In `lanes.js`:

- **Ruler** (the top bar, with the clip tracks): drag down to zoom in, up to zoom out, sideways to scroll; the moment you grabbed stays under the pointer. A click moves the playhead. Zoom goes from 0.25 to 32 times.
- **Lane names** (the left side): drag right for taller lanes, left for shorter (`tools.laneH`, 28 to 320px); drag up and down to scroll. A click on the empty part selects the whole lane; Shift-click adds lanes.
- The ruler and lane names are sticky; the grid scrolls both ways in `.sl-scroll` (two-finger trackpad scrolling is the browser's own). Pinch or ⌘/Ctrl + wheel zooms, Alt + wheel changes lane height, two fingers pan on touch screens.
- **Fine lines**: seconds inside each moment (seconds per moment from `opts.secondsPerMoment`), then halves, quarters... as you zoom; value lines per step of each lane's scale, thinned to 7px apart; a lane taller than 76px writes its scale beside its name.
- **Areas**: drag across empty space to select lanes by moments. Copy selection, then Paste into another selection (repeats to fill it; one lane fills several) or at the playhead. Onto a different curiosity, values keep their place on the scale. ⌘C/⌘X/⌘V/Delete/Esc work on it. `copyArea` and `pasteAreaCommands` are exported.
- **Area tools** (CapCut's Reverse and speed, for nodes): while an area is selected the toolbar shows **Reverse** (the stretch plays backwards: the last node comes first), **Flip** (each node's setting turned upside down on its own lane: low becomes high), **Stretch ×2** (the nodes spread out to take twice as long, from the area's first moment; nodes already in the way are replaced) and **Squeeze ½** (they take half as long). When two nodes land on one moment the later one stays; nothing goes past the film's last moment; joins follow their nodes. Each is one undo step. `reverseAreaCommands`, `flipAreaCommands` and `stretchAreaCommands` are exported. No keyboard keys yet.
- **Freeze and Shape ▾** (CapCut's Freeze frame and its Speed > Curve presets, used on settings instead of speed), also in the area toolbar. **Freeze** holds the setting each selected lane plays at the selection's first moment for the whole stretch: the nodes inside go, and a node at the first and the last moment carry that one setting, so nothing drifts between them. **Shape ▾** opens a small menu that rewrites each selected lane's settings over the selection between its own lowest and highest setting there: **Ease in** (slow start, fast finish), **Ease out** (fast start, gentle finish), **Rise and fall** (up to the highest in the middle and back down, like a montage), **Pulse** (a quick flash high in the middle), **Hold then jump** (flat, then the change all at once on the last moment, a hero moment) and **Build** (climbs in a few even steps). Ease in, Ease out, Hold then jump and Build go downward instead when the lane ends lower than it starts. Nodes go on whole moments only, and only where the shape bends. A lane that stays at one setting over the selection has nothing to shape and is left alone (the message says so). Joins to nodes that go are removed; joins to nodes whose setting changes keep in step. Each is one undo step, and the selection stays. `freezeAreaCommands(st, lanes, area)` and `shapeAreaCommands(st, lanes, area, preset)` are exported, with the `PRESETS` table (the curve `SHAPES` are for one line between two nodes, so the presets are their own table).
- **Suite clips** (CapCut's compound clip, and its way of saving things to use again): with an area selected, **Save as suite clip** in the area toolbar asks for a name in a small pop-up in the timeline (Enter saves, Esc cancels) and keeps the selection's nodes and joins as a named suite clip, a curiosity suite with its moves in time. Values keep their place on each curiosity's scale; lanes not on a track are left out. **Suite clips ▾** in the toolbar lists them: name, how many moments long, and the curiosities it holds in plain words, with **Drop at the playhead** (it lands starting at the playhead, moved earlier if it would run off the end, replacing what those lanes had there; one undo step, through `pasteAreaCommands`), **Drop as an analogy** (like the Prism's "Make it an analogy": each lane starts from the setting it has at the playhead and makes the clip's moves, step for step on its scale, stopping at the top or bottom), **Rename** and **Delete** (asks once more; your film is not changed). Each lane lands on the timeline's lane for the same curiosity; a curiosity the timeline doesn't show still goes into your film, on the track it is on or its usual track (the message names it, since only Arrange shows every lane); a locked lane is skipped and named. Suite clips are kept in `localStorage` key `curiosities-suite-clips-v1` as `[{ id, name, made, span, lanes, links, curiosities }]`, across films and reloads, and not in undo (they are not film data). Pure helpers on `CurioLanes`: `suiteClip(copyArea(...), name)`, `migrateSuiteClips`, `suiteClipSummary`, `suiteClipTargets`, `analogyClip(clip, starts)` and `dropSuiteClipCommands(st, clip, start, { shown, analogy })`; `suiteClips()` reads the saved list. The mounted lanes have `saveSuite(name)`, `suiteName()`, `suiteClips()`, `dropSuite(id, analogy)`, `renameSuite(id, name)` and `deleteSuite(id)`.
- **Moving an area** (CapCut: drag a group of clips along the timeline): press inside the selected area (on empty space, a line, or one of its white nodes) and drag sideways. The whole block snaps to whole moments and stays inside the film; a copy of its outline and nodes slides with the pointer while the originals fade. Let go and every node in it moves that many moments, in one undo step; whatever its lanes had where it lands is replaced (⌘Z brings it back), joins inside it come along, a join to a node outside stretches, and the selection follows the block. Hold Alt (Option) when you let go to copy instead (the originals stay). Dragging mostly up or down inside the area still changes a node or starts a new selection; a click still lets go of the area. `moveAreaCommands(st, lanes, area, moments, copy)` is exported.
- **Frames on My film's clip track**: once a moment is at least 40px wide, each of My film's moments shows a small storyboard frame, like the thumbnails on CapCut's main track (`opts.thumbs()` from `ui.js`, drawn as cached picture data so redraws stay fast).
- **Curves**: click a line between two nodes to pick it; double-click it or press Curves. Seven shapes and a bend, previewed on a fine grid; Apply writes the curve into the moments between as small curve points (kept in `localStorage` "curiosities-screen-curves-v1" for drawing and reopening). `mount(...).curves(segKey?)` opens it.

In `ui.js`, **curiosity windows**: ⧉ on every lane and Details row (or `CurioScreen.openWin(id)`) opens a floating window built from the curiosity's sliders, each with its control, key diamond, "+ lane" and a chart. Hand-made parts: Emotion's feeling pad and emotional road, Shot size's frames around a person, Comedy's joke timing (setup, payoff, Rule of three, Callback), Angle height's cameras around a person seen from the side, Camera move's pictures of each move, and color chips and main-color swatches for Black and white to full color.

## Lane heads: Off, Solo and Lock

Like the small buttons on CapCut's track headers (hide or mute a track, lock it) and an audio app's solo. Each lane's name has three small buttons in `lanes.js`. To save room they fold away until you hover over the lane's name or tab to them. They always show on touch screens, and whenever one is in use.

- **👁 Off**: turns the lane's automation off or back on (the engine lane's own `on`, one undo step). An off lane stops changing the film. Its nodes stay, drawn faded with a dashed line, and its name is crossed out.
- **S Solo**: plays only this lane's automation and turns every other lane off, in one undo step. Press it again and the others come back the way they were. What was on before is kept in the tools as `solo: { lk, was }`. If the film changed in between, only lanes that still exist are put back, and lanes made since are left alone. Soloing another lane keeps the first "before". The soloed lane has a yellow S and a yellow edge. Pure helpers: `soloCommands(st, laneKey, prev)` and `soloActive(st, solo)`.
- **🔒 Lock**: stops the timeline from changing that lane's nodes. Clicking can't add a node, and nodes can't be dragged, removed, split or curved. Area tools, Move, Remove and Paste skip a locked lane and say so in a short message. A locked lane can still be copied, turned off or soloed. A lock is a view setting, not film data. It is kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `locks: { "track|curiosity": true }`. A locked lane has a hatched background and a lit 🔒. Details' controls still write to a locked lane, because the lock covers the timeline only.

Off and Solo need a lane that has nodes (an engine lane). Lock needs the lane to be on a track. The mounted lanes also have `laneOff(lk)`, `solo(lk)` and `lock(lk)`.

## Lane groups: fold a whole category

Like CapCut's track groups. With many lanes the timeline gets long, so `lanes.js` puts the lanes under a header for each filmmaking category: the same categories Details is built from (`CurioLevels.categoryOf`), such as Camera, Feeling or Comedy. A group comes where its first lane did, so what you are looking through stays on top, and lanes keep their order inside it. With only one lane there is no header.

- **The header** shows the category's name, how many lanes it holds and how many of them have nodes ("Camera · 5 lanes · 3 with nodes"). Click the name (▾) to fold the group, and again (▸) to open it.
- **A folded group** is one thin row with a dot at every moment where any of its lanes has a node, so you can still see where things happen. A bigger dot means several lanes have a node there. Click a dot to move the playhead to that moment.
- **Fold all / Open all** in the timeline toolbar folds every group or opens them all.
- Folding changes nothing in your film and is not an undo step. It is a view setting kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `folds: { category: true }`.
- A folded group's lanes are not drawn, so an area you drag across the lanes leaves them out (and Copy, Paste, Move and the area tools with it). Folding or opening a group lets go of the selected area. Joins to a lane in a folded group are not drawn until it is opened.

Pure helpers: `laneGroups(lanes, { catOf, labelOf, hasNodes, folds })` and `foldDots(st, lanes)`. The mounted lanes also have `fold(category, folded?)`, `foldAll(folded?)` and `groups()`.

## Film lines: the inspiration film in each lane

The Prism idea, on the timeline: the film you are learning from is white light, and each lane is one of its colors. In every curiosity lane, `lanes.js` draws the inspiration film's own settings for that curiosity as a faint dashed purple line (the purple of its clip track) behind your nodes, so you can see at a glance where your film follows it and where it goes its own way.

- **Which film**: the inspiration viewer picked in the Player (click its Inspect). When your own film is picked, it is the first inspiration viewer. Pick another viewer, or another film in a viewer's list, and the lines redraw from that film. `ui.js` hands it in through the `inspiration` mount option as `{ name, beats: [{ values }] }`.
- **How the two films line up**: the inspiration film is stretched to your film's length, the same way Blend reads it. Your film's first and last moments meet the film's first and last beats, and the beats in between are spread evenly. When both films have the same number of moments, it is moment for moment. A setting carries on until the film changes it.
- **No setting, no line**: a lane whose curiosity the film never sets has no line, and the line starts only once the film first sets it.
- **Film lines** in the timeline toolbar shows or hides them. It starts on; the tooltip names the film. The choice is kept in `curiosities-screen-tools-v1` as `filmLines: false`.
- **Take from the film** (in the selected-area toolbar) is the Prism's "Copy it" for a stretch: the film's settings for the selected lanes and moments become nodes, snapped to each curiosity's scale. Where the film has no setting, your film is left as it is. Locked lanes are skipped (the message says how many); a lane not on a track yet is put on one. It is one undo step, and the selection stays.

Pure helpers on `CurioLanes`: `filmBeat(j, n, count)` (which beat moment j reads), `filmLine(beats, n, cur)` (the line's setting at each moment, or null) and `takeFromFilmCommands(st, lanes, area, beats)`. The mounted lanes also have `filmLines()` (the toggle) and `take()`.

## Keyframe jumps and the frame shape

Like CapCut's ◀ ◆ ▶: in Details, once a curiosity has nodes, small ◀ and ▶ sit either side of its key diamond. ◀ moves the playhead to the moment of its previous node, ▶ to its next; greyed when there is none that way. The diamond works as before. The windows keep the bare diamond (`keyNavBtns` in `ui.js` wraps `keyBtn` for Details rows only).

**Ratio** in the Player's transport bar is CapCut's Ratio menu: wide 16:9, vertical 9:16, square 1:1 or cinema 2.39. It is Canvas edges' "Frame shape" slider (`canvasFill.ratio` in `data/db-editing.js`), so picking one puts a node at the playhead, and it can change during the film. My film's frame in the Player takes that shape, no taller than the wide frame, with the storyboard picture cropped to fill it, centered (like reframing a shot); inspiration films keep their own shape.

## Guides over the picture

Like the guides CapCut's Player can show. **Guides ▾** sits next to Ratio in the Player's transport bar. Tick any mix of them; they are drawn over My film's frame only, in its frame shape (a vertical frame gets vertical guides), and never get in the way of a click on the frame. Hover over a guide in the menu for one line on what it is for.

- **Thirds**: two lines each way at a third and two thirds. Put the important thing where the lines cross; pictures feel more alive than when it's dead center.
- **Center cross**: a small cross in the exact middle.
- **Safe areas**: the action-safe box (90% of the frame) and the title-safe box (80%), labeled "keep action inside" and "keep words inside".
- **Golden ratio**: lines at about 38% and 62% each way, a little nearer the middle than thirds.
- **Where attention is**: the rest of the picture dims a little around a soft glow on the part the moment's attention sits on, with a line such as "Eyes on: the main character's face". It uses the momentum reading's family for the moment (`CurioAttention`, the same as the "Attention:" label). `CurioFrame` doesn't report where it drew things, so `guideSpot` works the spot out from the moment's values with frame.js's own placement rules. Shot size, camera height, how many people and a left composition decide where the main character's face and body are. An insert shot means the object. A feeling, a thought or a plot turn glows on the face. Movement, wardrobe and camera glow on the whole main character, voice on the speech balloon, music on the notes, set and light on the window, comedy on the gag or the laughs. Anything else glows on the middle of the picture. With no reading it glows on the main character's face, where the eye goes first. The glow is cropped with the picture in a non-wide frame, so in a vertical frame it can fall outside what is shown.

Guides are a view setting, not part of the film, and never an undo step. They are kept in `curiosities-screen-v1` as `guides: ["thirds", ...]`. An older save that kept `guides: true` (the single ⌘; thirds switch) loads as Thirds. ⌘; still turns the thirds guide on and off. `CurioScreen.guides` has `list()`, `on()` and `spot(values, family, cast)` for tests.

## Hooks for other threads

`CurioScreen.row()` is the playhead's moment; `CurioScreen.setRow(i)` moves it. `CurioScreen.on(fn)` is told `{ row, rows }` after every redraw and playhead move, and returns a function that stops it. `CurioScreen.addPanel({ id, label, place: "player" | "details" | "timeline", mount(el) })` docks a side panel (the momentum meter): the Screen makes a `.sc-dock` element in that place, mounts it once and keeps it across redraws. The clock uses the Momentum window's seconds per moment (`secondsPerPanel` in `curiosities-momentum-v1`, 3 by default).
