# Curiosities: a guide for beta testers

Thank you for trying Curiosities. It's an early version, so things will break. That's fine, and telling us when they do is the most useful thing you can do.

## What it is, in one minute

A scene in a film is made of hundreds of small choices: how close the camera is, whether it shakes, how warm the light is, how fast the cuts come, how scared someone looks, whether a joke lands. Curiosities calls each of those choices a **curiosity**. It's one thing you can look at and turn up or down.

The app lets you do four things:

1. **Make a scene by turning those choices up and down** and watch a comic-strip storyboard change as you go.
2. **Look at a film you love the way a prism splits light**, one curiosity at a time ("this scene gets closer and closer as it gets scarier"), and borrow that move for your own scene.
3. **Make the choices move by themselves**, like a knob that turns on its own. You can also play them live from a keyboard or a music controller.
4. **Flip through lots of storyboard panels** like a flip book.

You don't need to know any film words. Every page asks its question in plain language, and Library > Words explains every term.

## How to open it

- **On a computer (best):** open the link you were sent in Chrome or Edge. If you were sent a folder instead, open the file `index.html` inside it in Chrome or Edge.
- **On a phone:** it works, but the screen is small. Try it on a computer first if you can.
- **Safari and Firefox:** most things work. Plugging in a music keyboard or controller only works in Chrome and Edge.

Your work stays on your own computer, in this browser. Nothing is uploaded. To keep your work safe, use Library > **Save project**, which saves a `.curio` file you can open again later.

## A 15-minute tour

Try these in order. Each one should take a minute or two. If something confuses you, write down where.

1. **My film.** Change a few of the controls on the left, such as shot size, camera carry or emotion. Watch the comic strip on the right change.
2. **Save it as a storyboard.** Under the strip, press **Save into the storyboard**. Then press **Flip through** and use the left and right arrow keys, or the ◀ and ▶ buttons. **Play** flips through on its own.
3. **Open a workspace.** The buttons in the bar are workspaces, grouped as Camera, People, Look, Sound, Feeling, Comedy and Story. Open **Camera angle**.
   - **In my film**: change a panel's value in the grid. Press **See it in My film** to check that the strip changed.
   - **Automate**: press and hold **Hold to play** on one of the modules. The panels move on their own while you hold it. Let go and they stop.
4. **The Prism.** In the same workspace, scroll to **Cross-pollinate from a film**. Pick one of the made-up model scenes. Press **Copy it** on a row, then go back to My film and look at what changed.
5. **A feeling.** Open **Emotion** and then **Comedy**. Read the question at the top of each page. Does it make sense to you?
6. **Archetype.** Open **Archetype** in the Story group. You'll see a 3D map of character types. Drag it around.
7. **Curated films.** Library > **Curated films**. Open a model scene and look at it beat by beat.
8. **Share a film.** Library > **Share a film** > **Save as a trace file** on any film. That small file is how you'd send a film you traced to someone else. Load one with **Choose trace files…**.
9. **Save your work.** Library > **Save project**. Close the tab, open the app again, then use Library > **Open or new project** to bring your work back.

If you have a MIDI keyboard or pad, plug it in, open any **Automate** section, and press **Learn switch (note)** on a module. Then play a note.

## What we most want to hear

- **Where did you get lost?** Name the page and what you were trying to do.
- **Which words didn't make sense?** Copy them exactly.
- **What felt fun?** What would you do again tomorrow?
- **What did you expect to happen that didn't?**
- **Anything that broke:** a blank page, a frozen button, an error message, or something that looked wrong. A screenshot helps a lot.

Use the [feedback form](feedback-form.md). Answer as much or as little as you like.

## Things we already know about

- When automation stops, its last values can stay on the storyboard. Press **Clear** above the strip in My film to hand the panels back.
- On a phone, the bar of workspace buttons is long, so you need to scroll down after tapping one to see its page. A small **VCV** button sits in the bottom-left corner and can cover a line of text.
- The 3D character map needs an internet connection the first time it loads.
- The model scenes are made up for practice. They aren't real films.
- The Maya, Blender, DaVinci Resolve and Unreal connections aren't part of this beta.
