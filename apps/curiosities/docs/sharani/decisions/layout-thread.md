# Layout thread: decisions (the Screen, Final Cut Pro style)

Thread: "Main layout" (Jeremy, 2026-10-02 17:18Z onward). Code: `apps/curiosities/screen/`, branch `curiosities-screen`, draft PR into `curiosities-beta`. Jeremy's words: #18 to #22 in `jeremys-words.md`. Each line: what was decided, and why. Mark any you want reversed.

1. **One screen, called the Screen, with window layouts as options** (1, 2 or 3 windows, plus "+ Inspiration film" for more). Jeremy 17:20Z: the layouts are "different parameters of the same screen".
2. **Default: one inspiration film plus my film, side by side.** Jeremy 17:24Z asked for one plus one; side by side matches Final Cut Pro's browser and viewer, and leaves room for the timeline under them. "Stacked" is one click away.
3. **Any number of inspiration viewers**, each with its own film picker and ×; it can also go down to none (the one-window layout). Jeremy 17:24Z: "unlimited".
4. **The film in a viewer is a cheap animated storyboard** drawn from the curated film's values (no footage). Beta is storyboards first (Jeremy #10). Real video can replace the frame later without changing the rest.
5. **One inspector on the right, for whichever viewer you click** (like Final Cut Pro's), rather than an inspector per viewer: it keeps the screen calm at three or more viewers.
6. **Twelve major categories** in the inspector: Camera, Performance, Light & color, Set & background, Wardrobe, Music & sound, Feeling, Comedy, Effects, Story & character, Editing & structure, Page & panel. They group the database's 25 workspaces. Jeremy 17:19Z: major categories, "so it's not too overwhelming".
7. **Main curiosities first**: each category shows five (the ones with the biggest momentum "push", so the ones that drive the story most), plus anything you are looking through; "Show all" opens the rest. On an inspiration film, the curiosities the film actually uses come first.
8. **Fine distinctions fold inside each curiosity** (▸ with a count), not as separate rows.
9. **Controls by kind of slider**: two choices are a toggle, a list with no order is a dropdown, words in order are a stepped slider, a short number range (up to 10) is a knob, a long range is a slider, and every curiosity has a small chart of how it moves through the film. Jeremy 17:18Z asked for "sliders and toggles and knobs and charts".
10. **Controls for my film write a node at the playhead**, so every change is automation and one undo step (Jeremy #12).
11. **Controls for an inspiration film are read-only**; instead each curiosity has "Take into my film" with an amount.
12. **Blending**: each taken film pulls my film toward it by its amount; my film keeps the rest (two films at 50% meet halfway; one film at 30% moves it 30%). For a list with no order, the strongest film's value wins once the takes reach 50%. Written as nodes on every moment, one undo step. Jeremy 17:24Z: "a blend of the two".
13. **Upper-window controls** (my recommendation in the thread, built): play, step, scrub with marks where the selection happens, speed, a lens switch (Highlight, Overlay, Lens only, Off), the attention meter in the corner (momentum), and the inspector.
14. **Playheads are linked**: my film's moment drives every viewer; inspiration films follow proportionally (moment 4 of 8 shows the middle beat of any film). An unlinked mode can come later.
15. **"Looking through" sits in the top bar** with the four levels and a dropdown grouped by category, because it filters every viewer and the timeline at once.
16. **The timeline sits under the viewers in the Screen** and shows the lanes of whatever you are looking through. **Arrange** shows every automated lane, with viewers hidden by default (Jeremy: "I don't think we would need a movie to be in the top window") and a "Show viewers" tick box.
17. **Film clip tracks at the top of the timeline**, one per inspiration film (its beats stretched across your film) and one for your film's moments, like Final Cut Pro's video tracks. Click a clip to move the playhead there.
18. **Each Arrange track has a curiosity dropdown**; changing it moves the track's nodes to the new curiosity at the same place on its scale (low stays low). One undo step.
19. **Show all potential curiosities / suites** open category chips (and suite buttons per category) so you lay out one group of tracks at a time, rather than 333 tracks at once.
20. **A proximity is two nodes joined by a line**, made by dropping one node on a node in another lane. Stored as an engine link (so undo, saving and the fingerprint check cover it): leader = the earlier node, "when it is this value, the other becomes that value", within = the gap in moments, scope = the two moments.
21. **Joined nodes are a group**: moving or copying any node moves or copies every node joined to it and the lines between them. A group of several joins is a proximity suite. Jeremy 17:18Z: "if one of the nodes is moved or copied to a new scene, then they're both copied".
22. **Copy proximity / Paste at the playhead**, with the clipboard kept across films and reloads. Jeremy 17:22Z.
23. **Database proximities can still be added as whole-lane rules** ("Add this proximity to my film" in the Screen), through the engine's own pack import. Those have no nodes; the line drawing is for node-to-node joins.
24. **The Screen opens on start** (Jeremy: "default to a two-window screen"); "Back to the app" closes it and it stays closed until you open it again; the bar's Screen button and Library, Arrange reopen it; `?screen=0` skips it.
25. **New files only** (`screen/`); plus the one `index.html` line inside PR #22 itself (the project convention: a PR adding a folder carries its own script tag). Automated browser tests skip opening the Screen on start so other parts' tests that click the app keep working (`?screen=1` forces it). `CurioScreen.mountViewer(el, {...})` is ready so every workspace can put a viewer at its top (Jeremy 17:20Z: "a view window for every single screen"); wiring it into workspaces.js is also for the app thread.
26. **Suggested windows per category** (a "Use N windows" button in each open category): Camera 2, Performance 2, Light & color 3, Set & background 3, Wardrobe 3, Music & sound 2, Feeling 2, Comedy 3, Effects 2, Story & character 1, Editing & structure 2, Page & panel 1. Three where comparing two looks side by side helps most; one where the work is about your own story. Question 34 asks Sharani.
27. **The app's own look** (paper, ink, saffron), with dark viewers like an editing suite.

## CapCut becomes the main model (Jeremy, 2026-10-02 18:00Z; his words #23)

Branch `curiosities-screen-5`, draft PR #28 into `curiosities-beta`. These replace decisions 6, 13, 15, 16 and 27 where they differ.

28. **CapCut's four panels**: the library at the top left, the Player in the center, Details on the right, and the timeline full width along the bottom. The layout follows Jeremy's screenshots of CapCut Desktop. The Player sits in the middle (CapCut puts it between the media panel and Details) because the viewers are what the screen is for.
29. **CapCut's dark look**: a near-black ground, dark rounded panels with thin gaps, white text, and cyan for whatever is selected. Orange marks proximities and new things. The storyboard frames stay light so the pictures read clearly.
30. **The categories are the icon tabs** across the top of the library, as CapCut's Media, Audio, Text, Stickers, Effects, Transitions, Filters and Adjustment are. Each has a line icon.
31. **Sixteen categories instead of twelve.** The four new ones are Filters, Text, Transitions and Speed, CapCut's own tabs. Its Audio tab became our Music & sound (now called Audio, with the mix inside). Its Effects tab joins our Effects, with layers and masks inside. The frame tools (punch-in, mirror, steadying, canvas) go under Editing. Stickers sit inside Text. Templates and AI avatar are not categories; they are saved suites and later AI work.
32. **Left sidebar = the category's groups**: its workspaces, then Suites, Proximities and Proximity suites, as CapCut's pills (Yours, Library, Trending...). **The grid shows cards.** Clicking a card looks through it. Its cyan **+** puts it on the timeline, as CapCut's + adds media: a curiosity becomes a track, a suite puts all its curiosities on, and a proximity is added to the film. A search box looks across every category.
33. **Details follows the icon tab**: it shows the picked category for the picked viewer, instead of an accordion of every category. If what you are looking through also lives in other categories, buttons jump there.
34. **The Player holds the lens switch** in its header (CapCut's Player menu corner). The transport bar under the viewers has the timecode (one moment = one second, CapCut style 00:00:00:03), Play and speed, and the window layouts. This takes them out of the top bar, which keeps only the view, Looking through, and Back to the app (cyan, where CapCut puts Export).
35. **Viewers share one row in the Player** and shrink to fit, so three windows still fit without scrolling. Stacked is the column option.
36. **Editing curiosities from Final Cut Pro and CapCut**: 34 curiosities, 8 suites, 14 proximities and 3 proximity suites, in `screen/edit-curiosities.js`. Every editing setting either app offers (transition style and length, sound leading or lagging the cut, clip animations, filters with strength, exposure, warmth, texture, shot matching, on-screen text, captions, text style, stickers, clip speed, reverse and replay, freeze frames, jump cuts, cutting to the beat, pace across a scene, music ducking, audio fades, voice effects, sound hits, loudness, voice-over, overlays, blend modes, cutouts, masks, tracking, video effects, punch-ins, mirror and rotate, steadying, canvas edges) is a curiosity, each with sliders and a momentum note. Curiosities that already existed were not duplicated: Transition, cutting rate, shot length, speed ramp, color filter, saturation, aspect.
37. **They load before the app's database install** (one `index.html` line after `data/db-momentum.js`), so the app, the engine and saving know them by their own ids. Without that line they would be dropped on reload. The file is in the database's format and proposed for `data/`; the database thread decides where it ends up.
38. **The frame draws the new categories**: a filter tint and exposure, grain, a caption bar or title card, a "BAM!" sound word, stickers, a wipe or dissolve edge for transitions, and speed streaks, a slow-mo tag or a freeze border for speed.
