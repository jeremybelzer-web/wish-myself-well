## After your 11:15Z direction: everything is an automatable parameter

26. Built one shared automation layer (automation.js) before more Maya features. Every curiosity (157), suite (42), proximity (15) and proximity suite (5) is a parameter with a setting A, a setting B and a modulator between them. Reason: you said automation of these four levels is what the app is about, so it must be one system every tab uses, not a feature of one tool.
27. Modulators are an LFO (sine, triangle, square, saw, random; 0.05 to 10 Hz; depth), a manual knob, or a MIDI CC. Triggers are a MIDI note, a computer key or a button, in gate (hold) or toggle mode. Reason: your example was a performer triggering a parameter that then flicks between two settings at a rate they set; square LFO is the default because "flick" means a hard switch.
28. A suite's two settings are two suites (or none), a proximity's are off/on plus its delay in beats, and a proximity suite's are off/on plus a delay offset for all its members. Reason: that is the closest meaning of "two settings" at each level that still changes the scene.
29. Made five proximity suites from the countable proximities: the camera answers the body, emotion steers the lens, loudness and frame size, weather and surface, long-form shape. Reason: the fourth level needed real groups to start with; you can rename or regroup them.
30. Modular synths connect through MIDI, not a plug-in: each running parameter can send its position as a MIDI CC, and incoming CCs can drive any parameter. With a virtual MIDI port (IAC on Mac, loopMIDI on Windows), VCV Rack's MIDI-CC to CV and CV to MIDI modules talk to the app both ways. Reason: a static web page can use Web MIDI (Chrome, Edge) but cannot open OSC or audio-rate CV directly; MIDI CC is what VCV already speaks.
31. While anything is running, the automation result plays on the Board as an applied strand called "Automation". Reason: the Board is where a performer or student sees the scene change.
32. Added two top tabs: Automate (a patch bay of synth-style modules for every parameter, with MIDI learn, key binding and a scope) and Cross-pollinate (one game per level, each drawing on inspiration films from your studies). Reason: you asked that each automation category have a game that cross-pollinates from inspiration films, aimed at film students.
33. Any curiosity chip in a Studio tool can be clicked to open its automation module. Reason: "each curiosity must be automatable" should be true from wherever you meet it.
34. The Automate tab has a Performer view: big hold-to-play pads for every module you star, bind or run, each showing its binding and a meter. Reason: a dancer wearing MIDI needs a few large targets, not a full patch bay.
35. Live's MIDI mappings each get a "Make it an automation" button, and Live and Automate share one MIDI connection. Reason: two MIDI systems would confuse a performer; Live stays as the quick mapper and Automate is the home of automation.
36. In the Chain reaction game, if the board shows none of the chain's causes, the game sets the first link's cause (for example handheld) so the chain visibly fires. Reason: otherwise a student presses "Trigger the chain" and nothing happens.
37. Every Studio tool now follows running automations live (its controls move and show an "automated" badge). Reason: an automation should be visible wherever that curiosity lives, not only on the Board.

## After your 12:09Z feedback: on/off plus graded lanes, and the Prism

38. A and B are now the on/off switch. The trigger (MIDI note, key, button) turns a curiosity, suite, proximity or proximity suite on or off, and the old two settings became the main lane inside it. This replaces decision 26's "two settings is the whole parameter". Reason: you said a camera angle needs "how low to how high", not just two states.
39. Every parameter has its own lanes, each with a from, a to, a curve (straight, eased, slow start, slow end, steps) and its own modulator (follows the main one, LFO, knob or MIDI CC). A curiosity's lanes are its other dimensions (angle height also offers shot size, close to wide, and dutch). A suite has a lane per member. A proximity has cause, delay, how often and effect size. A proximity suite has a delay and how often per member. Every parameter also has an amount lane, the share of panels it plays in. Lanes start switched off so turning a parameter on does not change more than you asked. Reason: your note that each of the four levels "need to have their own automatable parameters".
40. Word curiosities are now graded through a scale instead of jumping: angle height runs floor, low, eye, high, overhead; shot size runs insert, close, medium, wide. Reason: "how low to how high" has to pass through the middle. Other word curiosities use their catalog order, which you can reorder.
41. Each automation plays in a moment, a span of panels in your film, and any lane can sweep across that moment, so one shot can go from low to high. Reason: "cross-pollinate them with any given moment in our film".
42. Any curiosity can be added as a lane to any parameter, and a MIDI control can be bound to a single lane. Reason: a performer may want one strap on "how high" and another on "how close".
43. "Amount" and "how often" use a fixed roll of the dice per panel, so the same panels are chosen each time instead of flickering. Reason: a filmmaker needs the result to be repeatable.
44. Added a Prism tab: pick a curated film (a study) and it splits into four bands (curiosities with the range the film used, suites it formed, proximities that held with their delays, proximity suites). Any row can be dropped onto a moment of your film as a ready automation with the film's own ranges, plus suggested next moves to develop it. Reason: your prism image, film in as white light and the app separating its named colors.
